Drowned continents: the transition's width

Deep Time master d3e8ade9, 22 October 2026. Measured only: nothing in the code changed.

The question: one V1 criterion asks that at most 0.25 of the continental crust lie under the sea. Master's median is 0.414. Why?

Mostly one setting, and it is yours. The ocean-continent transition is 200 km wide on the planet, which the plate model sees as 1,632 km. Making it 200 km wide on the model's own sphere cuts the drowned share to 0.279 and brings land and the largest landmass into their bands. That is D6.

Three findings

  1. 1.The target is Earth's, with no margin

    The model calls crust continental from where its transition is 20 km thick. Earth's crust at least 20 km thick lies 0.248 under the sea by water depth and 0.237 by rock height (CRUST1.0). Counted by Cogley's continent-ocean boundaries instead, Earth itself is 0.306 and would fail.

    Suggested beside the target: the share of crust at least 25 km thick that stands above the sea, which does not depend on where the model draws the edge. Earth is about 0.81, master 0.572.

  2. 2.The main cause: the plate model sees the transition 8 times too wide

    W, the width of the ocean-continent transition (PLT-262), is 200 km on the planet: your choice of 5 October, to keep Earth's slopes in the game's metres. The quick planet is 8.16 times smaller than the Earth-sized sphere the plate model runs on, so the model sees 1,632 km.

    • 97 to 100% of the continental crust lies within 100 km of ocean, inside the taper's continental half.
    • That half thins the crust to 20 to 33 km, all under the 35.1 km at which the surface reaches sea level, so it stays drowned unless something thickens it.
    • Passive margins have no shelf: the median shelf is 0 km wide (Earth's passive margins 84 to 88 km), and the 200 m isobath is 5 km from the shore (Earth 25).

    A correction to the record. When you chose 200 km, the cost weighed against a narrower W was 4.5° slopes. That was the figure for W = 0, with the transition off. A narrower W costs much less: 3.2° instead of 2.5° at 200 km on the model sphere (Earth 2.1°).

  3. 3.Secondary: the ocean keeps all its sediment, and the sea ends high

    • The ocean holds 5 to 6 times Earth's sediment (GlobSed). Two to three times Earth's inflow arrives, and none of it subducts: it rides into a trench and comes back on the overriding plate.
    • Sending 80% of it to the mantle lowers the sea by about 720 m but leaves the share drowned (0.412). The continents shrink with the sea, because arcs add no new crust; on Earth they add about as much as subduction takes.
    • The sea's final height reads high: the forced last resampling adds 48 to 118 m over the last step's sea, in all 12 runs.

1 decision for you

The recommended option is first. Answer "D6 a", or together with the volcano page's, like "D1 to D6 as recommended".

D6. The transition's width

Your choice of 5 October: 200 km on the planet, to keep Earth's slopes in the game's metres.

  • aRecommendedW = 200 km on the model sphere. The drowned share falls from 0.414 to 0.279, and land and the largest landmass come into their bands. The cost is passive slopes of 3.2° instead of 2.5°. It changes the planet, so test worlds get regenerated.
  • bKeep W at 200 km on the planet.
  • cThe bigger change, if the slope matters more: the crust tapers in the model's frame, and the map and the surface draw the margin wider, in the planet's.

Still open, not decisions

Even with D6 (a) the drowned share stays at 0.279, above 0.25. Still to fix:

  • The sediment loop: sediment can subduct only once arcs grow new crust.
  • The end bias: the last resampling lifts the final sea.
  • Passive margins with no shelf.
  • Land above 2 km and coasts with high ground, at about twice their bands.
  • Collisions, under 2 per 100 Myr (the band is 2 to 6).
