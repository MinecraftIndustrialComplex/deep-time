How the wrap works

The overworld is a strip of land 16,384 blocks wide. Its east edge is joined to its west edge, so if you walk east for long enough you end up back where you started. The join is called the seam. You never see it. This page shows where it is and what happens when you cross it.

An invented planet: the terrain is decoration; the seam, grid and bands are real.Drag to turn
Where it is
Along the line x = 8,192, which is the same line as x = −8,192. It runs the whole way from the north pole to the south pole, on the opposite side of the planet from x = 0. Spawn goes wherever Deep Time finds the best starting spot, so it can be anywhere, even next to the seam.
How you get across
You just keep walking. The server keeps a single copy of the world, x from −8,192 to 8,191. Anything that steps past one end is moved to the other end in the same tick. Your game never sees that jump.
What your coordinates do
F3's XYZ line shows where the server stores you, so it jumps from 8,191 to −8,192. Toroidal World adds an Unwrapped XYZ line showing your game's own count. That one keeps counting up: after one lap east it reads 16,384.

Crossing the seam, seen by the server and by your game

What your game sees

One strip with no edge, centred on you. A tag like = −512 means the chunk is stored at the other end.

What just happened

    On foot, your game moves you and reports your position. The server moves its copy of you across as soon as that report lands past the edge, and sends nothing back. A minecart is moved by the server: at the end of its tick it jumps across with you in it. A Sable ship jumps across as one piece when its centre crosses, not its bow. Until then the front of the hull sticks out past the east edge (dashed outline). In every case your game just gets the next position, one step further east.

    Everything else

    Everything else that crosses

    Toroidal World handles each of these in its own code. The label on each card says whether we have seen it work on a test server or only read it in the code.

    Mobs keep chasing you

    A mob that crosses is moved at the end of its own tick. Its path and the spot it was walking to are moved by the same amount, so a zombie chasing you across the seam doesn't lose you.

    Code

    Vehicles take their riders

    A minecart or boat is moved together with everyone riding it, in the same tick. A boat or horse you steer is moved when its movement report crosses, the same way you are on foot. Speed is kept.

    Code

    Sable ships stay in one piece

    The whole ship, and anything joined to it, moves across when its centre does. On a test server, a 10-block jet at 40 blocks/s crossed 20 times out of 20 and kept its speed (38.6 blocks/s after). A hull saved while straddling the seam came back intact.

    Distances go the short way

    Mob AI, pathfinding, arrows and snowballs, explosions, sounds and particles all measure to the nearest copy. A creeper three blocks across the seam is three blocks away. On the test server, a snowball thrown east from x = 8,188 was stored at about x = −8,187 a moment later: just past the seam, at the other end of the strip.

    Snowball: test serverThe rest: code

    Compasses point the short way

    A compass or lodestone compass points at the nearest copy of its target. If spawn or your lodestone is closer through the seam, the needle points through the seam.

    Code

    Commands take stored coordinates

    Coordinates you type have to be inside the stored strip. /tp @s 9000 70 0 is refused with 9000 is outside this world — it must be between -8192 and 8191. ~ offsets work anywhere. /fill and /clone refuse a box that crosses the seam, so you run them once for each side.

    Seen with /forceloadRest: code

    Maps fold too

    Player and banner markers on vanilla maps use stored coordinates. Toroidal World lists Xaero's Minimap, Xaero's World Map, JourneyMap and FTB Chunks as supported: they file map tiles by stored coordinates and draw the repeats.

    Code and TW's supported-mods list

    The ground doesn't break

    Deep Time's terrain repeats exactly every 16,384 blocks, so hills, rivers and ore continue straight through the seam. On the test server, terrain, a tree and a village house built across the seam all came out whole.

    Seen on a test server

    The save holds one copy

    Region files exist only for the stored strip, r.−16 to r.15 along x. After generating a square across the seam, no region file outside that range held a chunk.

    Seen on a test server (16k and 32k)

    Mods that work out positions or distances in their own code don't get any of this automatically. Toroidal World patches the ones on its supported list. Others, such as Simple Voice Chat's positional audio, may break near the seam. That hasn't been tested.

    Other dimensions

    The Nether loops at exactly 1:8

    The Nether is 2,048 blocks round, exactly one eighth of the overworld, and its seam sits at x = ±1,024, directly under the overworld's. One Nether block still stands for 8 overworld blocks everywhere, including across both seams, so portals near the seam link up the same way they do anywhere else. The End doesn't loop at all: it's vanilla.

    Set in Deep Time's code and Toroidal World's portal codeNot yet probed on a server

    Close up on both seams

    Two portals 16 blocks either side of the overworld seam are 32 blocks apart through it. They link to Nether spots 2 blocks either side of the Nether seam, 4 blocks apart: 32 ÷ 8. Toroidal World refuses to start a world whose Nether width isn't exactly the overworld width divided by the portal scale.
