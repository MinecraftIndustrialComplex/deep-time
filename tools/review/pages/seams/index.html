How Deep Time's seams work

Deep Time plays a whole planet on Minecraft's flat block grid. East–west it loops: walk 16,384 blocks east and you are back where you started, with no edge anywhere. North–south it cannot loop, so the poles get a soft cap. Every figure below is interactive, and every number follows the planet size picked in the bar above.

East–west: a seamless loop
The overworld is a cylinder in x, closed by the Toroidal World mod. Mountains, rivers and biomes run straight through the seam; mobs, items and ships cross it.
North–south: a soft cap
From 75° latitude a pushback toward the equator grows, with a warning and a storm. At 80° it becomes a hard stop.
Later: pole transit
Crossing a pole may one day teleport you to the opposite longitude, heading south. Not built yet.

Plan §5.1, §5.2

The planet, unrolled

The overworld is an equirectangular map of a small planet: x is longitude, z is latitude. A block is about 300 m across and 20 m tall, and the map is C blocks around and C/2 blocks from pole to pole, so each block of z is 0.02197° of latitude. The continents below are a made-up example planet; the grid, bands and numbers are the real ones.

longitude = 360° · (x + C/2) / C        x from −C/2 (0°) to C/2 − 1 (just under 360°)
latitude  = −z · 360° / C               north is −z; the pole edges are z = −C/4 and z = C/4
  • Seam, x = ±C/2
  • Soft cap, 75°–80°
  • Past the hard stop, 80°–90°
  • Beyond the pole edge: ice-sheet ocean

Drag sideways to spin the planet: the terrain never ends. Point or tap for coordinates; arrow keys move the pin (Shift for bigger steps).

Why the far north looks smeared. Planet-scale features (continents, mountain belts) are sampled at their true latitude and longitude, so on this map they are stretched east–west by sec φ: 1.0 at the equator, 2.0 at 60°, 3.9 at 75°. Erosion, rivers and valleys are computed in block space, so they keep their shape wherever they are. The stretch near the poles is one more reason for the caps. Tick Stretch to see circles that are 10° across on the planet.

Plan §7.10 · Toroidal World 0.19.3

The seam, up close

The server stores exactly one copy of the planet: x from −8,192 to 8,191. Nothing past it exists on disk. The Toroidal World mod (TW) makes that edge disappear in two ways: anything that crosses it is moved back into the span in the same tick, and each player is sent the copy of the world nearest to them.

Below is a top-down strip of the two ends of the span, one pixel square per block. Launch something east across the seam, then flip between what the server stores and what a player standing next to the seam is sent.

Numbers above the strip are chunk x, below it block x; a magenta number with * is a chunk sent to you at a shifted position. Drag in the strip to walk (across the seam only in the player's view); ← → keys step one block.

Server log

    Crossing, on the server
    A mob, item, projectile, minecart or ship entity that ends its tick at x ≥ C/2 is moved to x − C in the same tick (x < −C/2 → x + C), with its passengers, velocity and pathfinding target. Players are moved when their movement packet crosses. Coordinates therefore stay inside the stored span.
    What players are sent
    Chunks, entity positions and block updates are rewritten to the copy nearest each player, so there is no visible edge. Distances for mob AI, sounds, projectiles, explosions and compasses are also measured to the nearest copy: the world behaves round, not just looks round.
    Terrain continuity is Deep Time's job
    Every simulation raster wraps in x (cell index modulo width) and the sub-cell detail noise has period C, so column −C/2 continues column C/2 − 1 exactly. The river and the rail in the strip run straight through.
    Chunk keys
    A chunk request past the edge is folded to the stored chunk by TW. Deep Time code folds a position with TW's ToroidalShape.fold before using it as a key.
    How Deep Time shapes its world
    TW's own shape picker runs only from its Create World button, never on a dedicated server, so a mixin on WorldDimensions.bake calls TW's public ShapeDimensions.withSpans. Deep Time's generator subclasses NoiseBasedChunkGenerator, so TW stamps it (same object, shape attached) and wraps its noise-fill and biome passes.
    World border, structures, fallback
    Along x the border never gets in the way once it covers the loop; along z it is vanilla, but the pole cap limits z first. TW moves structure starts across the seam (test pending). Had TW failed, Deep Time would teleport entities at the seam itself; not needed so far.
    One subtlety from the tests
    TW folds movement: an entity that steps over the border is moved. A position set directly past the edge (a teleport) is not folded by that rule, so the seam tests move entities under their own motion (STATUS.md, P0-06 notes).
    Not checkable without a client
    How crossing looks and feels (a hitch, pop-in, a ship's hull staying whole on screen), entity tracking across the seam in multiplayer, and how contraptions render at the seam. The player's view above is TW's documented behaviour, not something anyone has watched yet.

    wrapped_planet_worlds.md §5.1 · plan §7.10, §7.11

    Round one way, capped the other

    Roll the stored span east–west and its two ends meet: that is the cylinder TW plays, and every row of blocks closes on itself. The real planet is a sphere, where each polar row, C blocks long, would have to shrink to a single point. The block grid cannot do that, and closing the pole edge any other way makes a mirror.

    Plan §7.11 · built in P1-18 (prototype in P0-06)

    The poles: a soft cap, then a wall

    From 75° a pushback toward the equator grows with latitude, an action-bar warning appears, and a storm (fog and particles, drawn by the client) thickens. Sable ships get wind: their velocity is pushed toward the equator. At 80° the pushback becomes a hard stop. Terrain exists all the way to the pole edge; past it (|z| ≥ C/4), chunks that load at view distance are an ice-sheet ocean.

    BuiltP1-18. The curves are the real ones, with s the depth into the 75°–80° band: pushback 0.10·s1.5 blocks/tick² (at 80° it cancels walking), storm s0.7, Sable wind up to 20 blocks/s². Creative players get the storm and the stop but no wind; spectators only the storm. The warning texts are the real ones; the scene itself is an illustration of what the client draws.

    Plan §5.1, §7.10 · wrap/PlanetShape.java

    Nether and End

    The Nether loops too, at exactly 1:8: 128 chunks against the overworld's 1,024, so one Nether block still stands for 8 overworld blocks everywhere, including across both seams, and portals stay consistent. TW requires the Nether width to be exactly the overworld's divided by 8 (and at least 16 chunks). The End is vanilla: it does not loop.

    // wrap/PlanetShape.java, called from the mixin on WorldDimensions.bake
LoopSpans spans = LoopSpans.ofWidth(Direction.Axis.X, chunks);        // chunks = C / 16
return ShapeDimensions.withSpans(dims, spans, spans.scaledDown(NETHER_SCALE),   // NETHER_SCALE = 8
        LoopSpans.NONE, GenerationOptions.DEFAULT);                     // End: no loop

    The End: no loop

    LoopSpans.NONE: the End keeps vanilla's shape, with no seam and the usual world border.

    Not probedThe Nether and End spans are set in code; the probe run so far covered the overworld only.
