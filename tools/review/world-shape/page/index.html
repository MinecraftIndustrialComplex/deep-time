Why a cube can beat a pillowcase

Glue flat squares of block grid together with slides, half-turns and quarter-turns until they cover the planet once. Walking round almost any point you turn 360°. At a few corners you turn less, and by Gauss–Bonnet the missing angles always add up to 720°. Since every glue is a multiple of 90°, a corner can miss 90° or 180° (or 270°, worse).

  • Pillowcase (Peirce, Guyou, Petroff, Gringorten, Collignon): 4 corners × 180° missing. Half-turn seams make it possible.
  • Cube (Moser cube, QSC, HEALPix, the graphics warps): 8 corners × 90° missing. Only quarter-turn seams make it possible, which Immersive Portals and the wrap engine both allow.

The less a corner misses, the less any map must distort round it. An equal-area map must stretch shapes to at least 2π/θ near a corner of angle θ: 2× for the pillowcase, 1.33× for the cube. The best measured maps reach 2.67× (Petroff) and 1.42× (Moser cube). A conformal map must instead blow up area: like r⁻² round a pillowcase corner, only r^−⅔ round a cube corner.

What a player walks through

Every seam is a rigid motion of the block grid, so a straight walk stays straight across every seam; only the heading in raw coordinates changes, and the portal or engine hides that. The maps show two straight walks per world: once round the equator, and north over a pole down to the equator on the far side.

Where the corners land on this planet

The bake can turn the planet about its axis to keep corners off land. On an equal-area cube a corner magnifies nothing, so this matters less than for the conformal Peirce/Guyou map, where the four points must be at sea; it still keeps three-way portal corners away from builds.
