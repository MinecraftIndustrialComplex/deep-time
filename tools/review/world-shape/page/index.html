Petroff–Guyou or the Moser cube

Two seamless, equal-area worlds at today's scale: Petroff–Guyou, Petroff's 2021 square map in Guyou's 2:1 layout (two hemisphere squares, one slide, two half-turn seams), and the Moser cube, the equal-area cube in the cross net (six faces, one slide, four quarter-turn and two half-turn seams). This page works through what players do and feel in each, and draws what happens at a corner of each from the real geometry. Every picture is computed on the test VM from the projection survey's own code, with a real 16k bake (earthlike_quick_16k, workbench run 1a103bb2d2b) resampled onto both worlds.

The short answer

Recommended The Moser cube, cross net, today's scale, with its eight corners placed at sea where the planet allows and kept clear of building

The cube draws continents with about half the stretch (on this planet of land is stretched more than 1.5×, against on Petroff–Guyou), puts both poles in the middle of a face instead of on a seam, has no creases, keeps walking distances closer to the planet's, and its faces can be the sphere-native grid the solvers move onto (the research's recommendation; the climate grid itself is still Ben's open choice). It pays at its corners: eight of them instead of four, and unlike Guyou's they cannot be drawn as one consistent picture (below). That cost is real, new on this page, and narrows the gap; it does not reverse it.

What would flip it to Petroff–Guyou: if a single, consistent picture at every corner matters more than shape (Distant Horizons, vanilla maps and the wrap engine's band all see a tear at each cube corner), if Immersive Portals is to stay the long-term route (four portals against fourteen, quarter-turn portals untested), or if the plane-covering client is ever wanted (a cube rules it out for good). See the full case for each side.

Corners up close

Both worlds are flat everywhere except at a few corners (cone points), where the angle round the point is short. Gauss–Bonnet makes the missing angles add up to 720°, so Guyou has 4 corners each missing 180°, and the cube 8 corners each missing 90°. A player can walk right up to one: it is a place in the world.

The figures below are computed from the real maps at one corner of each world: Guyou's at the midpoint of its top edge (where the two hemisphere squares meet the north half-turn seam), the cube's where band faces F0 and F1 meet the north face N. For these close-ups only, each world is turned so a coast runs past the corner (Guyou λc = °, cube λ₀ = °), to show what a corner on land looks like; the turns chosen for the worlds above keep the corners at sea.

Plane tiling: Ben's point

  • The pillowcase is a flat orbifold. 180° divides 360°, so copies of the Guyou world, some turned 180°, tile the whole plane with no gaps or overlaps (the group p2). Measured: neighbouring pixels of the tiling below are neighbours on the planet everywhere ( torn pixels, largest step blocks at blocks a pixel).
  • The cube's 270° corners do not divide 360°. Round each corner the fourth quarter has two candidate pictures (striped below); there is no tiling of the plane.
  • Neither Immersive Portals nor the wrap engine needs a tiling. Portals redraw through each seam; the engine folds positions edge by edge. Both work on the cube.
  • The cube permanently rules out the plane-covering client route: a client that lives in the unwrapped plane (Toroidal World's design, extended with turns) needs the world to tile the plane. Guyou keeps that door open.
  • Can a corner render as one consistent picture? Not as a picture where every place appears once, in either shape: near a Guyou corner every place appears twice. But Guyou's doubled picture is consistent (no tear), so one raw copy serves every viewer; the cube's cannot be (a tear in the traced view, the band, the generator's outside and DH). The page's measurements make this sharper than "neither can".

Gameplay, topic by topic

Badges: run seen in a prototype or GameTest; computed measured here from the geometry and the real planet; inferred reasoned from code or docs; unknown not tested by anyone. "v1" is the production world joined by Immersive Portals (Ben's choice for the first version), "the engine" Deep Time's own wrap engine (Phase 1 and the Create adapters built, dev-only).

Travel

Petroff–Guyou

  • East–west along the equator: one straight line, blocks round (20 % short of the planet's 16,384), through one slide; no turn, no corner. The scale swings from about 30 blocks a degree at a hemisphere centre to 46 at its edge (chart) computed.
  • Over a pole: walking north up a hemisphere's centre line you reach the pole exactly on the half-turn seam, cross it, and walk south down the far meridian, as over a real pole; F3 yaw jumps 180° run (prototype). From anywhere else on the equator you cross the same seam beside the pole.
  • Diagonal (45° from spawn, 24,000 blocks): see the walk table; slides and half-turns only, F3 yaw jumps 180° at each half-turn computed.
  • Long boat trips: any course due east or west crosses only the slide, for ever. Every other course meets the half-turn edges, more often the steeper it is (table) computed.
  • Facing after a seam: slide 0°, half-turn 180°. You keep walking forward; only F3 and the compass notice.

Moser cube

  • East–west along the equator: one straight line, blocks round (8 % short), through one slide and three ordinary face edges; 45.5 blocks a degree at face centres, 17 % fewer at face edges computed.
  • Over a pole: from F0 you enter N through an ordinary edge, cross the pole in the middle of the face (no seam there), and leave through N's far edge, a half-turn seam onto F2, heading south. From F1 or F3 you enter and leave N through quarter-turn seams: two seams, F3 yaw +90° then +90° computed.
  • Diagonal (45° from spawn, 24,000 blocks): quarter-turns appear; each turns F3's yaw by 90° and swaps which coordinate is changing computed.
  • Long boat trips: due east or west inside the band crosses only the slide. Courses that leave the band meet quarter-turn and half-turn seams; at most headings the cube's straight courses cross more seams per 10,000 blocks than Guyou's (table) computed.
  • Facing after a seam: slide 0°, quarter-turn ±90°, half-turn 180°.
Unknown: boats and player-ridden vehicles across a turning seam. The Immersive Portals prototype sent items, minecarts and arrows across, not boats; the engine carries vehicles with passengers but leaves player-driven vehicles to Phase 2 (wrap-engine.md §10.4). With the engine, a turning crossing reloads chunks for a second or two on a vanilla client until the optional client re-anchor exists inferred; Immersive Portals shows no reload.

Navigation

Petroff–Guyou

  • Compass and north: F3's north (−z) is within 15° of true north on of the planet; it is good through the middle of each square and fails toward the poles (which sit on the top and bottom edges) and the corners. The sun always rises toward raw +x inferred (vanilla sky).
  • A vanilla compass points at spawn in raw coordinates: from spawn, it is more than 90° off the true way back on of the planet (map layer "Compass error") computed.
  • Of the two, Guyou's F3 north is right more often (within 15° on against ), and its compass misleads less, because the cube gives a third of the planet (its polar faces) to places with no consistent north computed.
  • F3 coordinates are continuous inside the world; at the slide x jumps by 13,088, at a half-turn x changes sign and z reflects about the edge (table) computed.

Moser cube

  • Compass and north: F3's north is within 15° of true north on of the planet. It is good on the band faces; on the polar faces true north points at the face centre, so F3's north is right only on one line through the pole and wrong by up to 180° elsewhere.
  • A vanilla compass: more than 90° off the true way back on of the planet computed.
  • F3 coordinates: at the slide x jumps by 15,104; at a quarter-turn x and z swap roles (walking north, you suddenly see x falling); at a half-turn both reflect (table) computed.

Vanilla maps and map walls (both)

  • A map item draws raw chunks. Past a seam it draws what is stored at those raw positions: with Immersive Portals the hidden outside (generated terrain that matches the far side but none of its player edits), with the engine the band's turned copies (player edits included, out to the band's depth) inferred.
  • A map wall that crosses a turning seam does not continue: the far side's own maps are turned 180° (Guyou, and the cube's half-turns) or 90° (the cube's quarter-turns) relative to the near side's. On the cube's polar faces a north-up map is not north-up on the planet.

Minimaps, waypoints, /tp and shared coordinates (both)

  • MIC ships no minimap or claim mod (pack mod list read 2026-10-04). Xaero-style minimaps draw loaded chunks and store waypoints in raw coordinates, so a waypoint 50 blocks away across Guyou's north seam at x = −2,000 shows about 4,000 blocks away; across one of the cube's quarter-turns near a corner the raw distance is small, across its half-turns thousands inferred. How they behave with Immersive Portals is unknown.
  • Every block has exactly one stored coordinate, so coordinates shared between players are unambiguous. /tp to a raw position beyond an edge lands in the hidden outside with portals; with the engine the entity is carried to the stored block after that tick inferred (§9.5).
  • Raw distance is not walking distance near seams; the distance table below gives the spread from spawn.

Building near seams and corners

Petroff–Guyou

With Immersive Portals (v1)
  • Four global portals: 13,088 blocks of turning seam and 6,544 of slide. Collisions within a block or two of a seam come from the hidden outside, which nobody sees (the prototype's invisible ice wall at the pole) run.
  • Water, lava and redstone stop at the portal plane; caves, ores and trees are cut at it run. Contraptions and Create networks do not cross.
  • The poles are on the seam: a base at the north pole sits on a portal.
  • Four corners in six places of the net; the 2026-10-02 prototype measured 30–40 % of frame rate at a portal corner and once escaped the world walking exactly into one run (not re-tested).
With the engine (later)
  • Blocks, water, redstone, light and entities cross half-turns; the spike's test site was built round a half-turn point, i.e. a Guyou corner run.
  • Create: shafts and cogs connect across, speed sign flipped; pumped water reaches a tank across a half-turn run. Belts, chain conveyors, gantries, track within a block of a seam and contraptions reaching across are refused run.
  • Corners are locally seamless in the engine: its unit tests check the local isometry on the whole Guyou layout, corners included read in WrapTopologyTest's description.

Moser cube

With Immersive Portals (v1)
  • Fourteen one-way portals: 22,656 blocks of turning seam and 3,776 of slide, 1.7× Guyou's turning seam, with the same hidden-outside collisions and the same stops for fluids, redstone and caves.
  • Quarter-turn portals are untested in Immersive Portals (the rotation NBT is inferred).
  • The poles are ordinary points in the middle of N and S: nothing special to build on.
  • Eight corners in fourteen places of the net, each a three-way meeting of portals frame cost unknown.
With the engine (later)
  • Blocks, water, redstone, light and entities cross quarter-turns too, at a test site built round a bounded quarter-turn corner run.
  • Create: a z shaft line crossing a quarter-turn lands as an x line, turning correctly run; fluids across a quarter-turn untested. Same refusals.
  • Near each corner blocks are not locally seamless (diagonals differ by who asks; figure 5): the engine's tests leave those neighbourhoods out, and Create's adapter refuses one-way diagonal links found there run. A small keep-out round each of the 8 corners is the honest fix inferred.
Known gap in both: with the MIC set, 6 of the engine's 15 Phase 1 tests fail (MIC's Alternate Current replaces redstone propagation, KubeJS sends a payload to the test player, a band reload times out) run; and an intermittent boot failure with the engine and MIC remains.

Exploring: the corners

Petroff–Guyou: 4 corners

  • At ±45° latitude on the two meridians 90° from the hemisphere centres: the midpoints of the top and bottom edges, and the rectangle's corners (where the slide meets the half-turns).
  • All four in open sea within 3° for of the possible turns λc on this planet computed.
  • At one: no magnification (equal-area), but shapes are forced to stretch at least 2× near it (2.67 at worst). A straight coast through the corner bends by about 90° on the map; a ring round it is half as long as normal.

Moser cube: 8 corners

  • At ±35.3° latitude, every 90° of longitude.
  • All eight in open sea within 3° for only of the turns λ₀ computed. On an equal-area cube a corner magnifies nothing and stretches shapes only to 1.33× (1.42 at worst), so terrain at one looks almost normal; what makes it matter is the tear (figures 3 and 4), not the distortion.
  • At one: a straight coast bends by about 45°; a ring round it is three quarters as long as normal.

Distant Horizons

Petroff–Guyou

  • DH draws raw chunks, and beyond them Deep Time's FarTerrain. Beyond every edge the raw world is the plane tiling, so far views across seams and across corners are right everywhere, with no tears (the tiling figure) computed.
  • Near a corner the far view shows the doubled picture: the terrain behind you again beyond the corner.
  • FarTerrain must fold at the two long edges: a few dozen lines (seamless-wrapping.md §7).

Moser cube

  • Beyond the edges the raw world needs a fold rule; with the engine's own edge order every corner throws a tear into the far view, and the polar rows' outside regions hold whichever face the first edge picks (the raw-surroundings figure) computed.
  • FarTerrain must fold quarter-turns and the cross: the same principle, more cases.

Both: Immersive Portals does not draw DH's LODs through portals (2026-10-02 prototype), so from inside vanilla's render distance on, a far view across a seam is whatever is stored at the raw positions beyond it. DH's world curvature is on by default with the planet's radius; the same for both.

Climate and biome bands

Petroff–Guyou

  • The equator is a straight line; parallels bow away from it toward the hemisphere edges, and near each pole they become half-circles round the pole point, cut by the half-turn seam (layer "Latitude bands").
  • A latitude band is not straight anywhere except the equator itself.

Moser cube

  • The equator is straight; parallels bow across each band face, less than Guyou's; above about 35° they leave the band faces and run round each polar face as rounded squares about the pole.
  • The polar and boreal bands live on N and S, centred and whole.

Deep Time's climate is computed on the sphere and looked up by true latitude per block, so bands are always right on the planet; this is only how they lie in block coordinates.

Felt distortion

Shapes up to 2.67× against 1.42× (survey). On this planet's actual continents (layer "Shape", coasts in white):

Continents stretch most toward the four corners and along each square's diagonals; a round island near a corner is drawn as an ellipse more than twice as long as it is wide.

Stretch is mild everywhere and peaks at the eight corners; nowhere is land drawn more than 1.42× too long.

Grid and tools

Petroff–Guyou

  • Hemisphere squares of b = 6,544 = 409 chunks; not region-aligned (6,544 / 512 = 12.8).
  • New: the engine's WrapLayouts.guyou centres the rectangle and is chunk-aligned only when h is a multiple of 32. 6,544 is not (204.5 × 32): today's scale needs b = 6,528 or 6,560 (0.25 % off), or an equator 8 blocks off z = 0 computed.
  • The workbench keeps the familiar 2:1 picture; vanilla maps north-up are close to north-up through the middle band.

Moser cube

  • Faces of 3,776 = 236 chunks; every edge on a chunk line and every turn centre on a chunk corner; not region-aligned (4,096 would be).
  • Toroidal World's x period becomes 15,104 = 944 chunks; whether TW is happy with a period that is not a multiple of 512 is unknown.
  • The workbench draws the cross (half the 4:3 box empty) or six faces; nothing forces the map to be the world, so it may keep drawing the planet in any projection.

Mod compatibility

Checked: Toroidal World does keep a slide on the cube cross net. The cross net's equator band is one slide, the band's east–west wrap, and the engine already has the layout for it: WrapLayouts.cubeCross(s, depth, slides = false) leaves that slide to TW, and its quarter-turns onto F3 carry the 4s translation themselves, so they never rely on TW. The statement that TW owns no edge holds only for the 3 × 2 net. Two caveats: TW's wrap is world-wide in z, so in the polar rows (mostly outside the net) it is consistent only if the engine folds before TW does (wrap-engine.md §4.8 assumes this) inferred; and TW's share is smaller, 3,776 of 26,432 blocks of seam against Guyou's 6,544 of 19,632.

The simulation grid

From docs/research/notes/sphere-native-grids.md (2026-10-04) and Ben's answers since:

  • Patches of regular arrays cost what the Lambert raster costs. On Deep Time's L1 cell count a diffusion pass on six cube faces with one ghost ring costs 0.89 ns a cell against Lambert's 0.88 (one thread), and a flow-routing pass 14.1 against 14.4. An unstructured Fibonacci mesh costs 3–10× more per stencil pass run (benchmark).
  • In true distances the cube is faster. An implicit diffusion solve needs 785 conjugate-gradient iterations on Lambert's stretched polar cells against 138 on the cube (1,273 ms against 249 at L1) run.
  • Ben's open choice for the climate grid: the equal-area cube or HEALPix. They run at the same speed; the equal-area cube distorts shapes to 1.42× (Moser) against HEALPix's 2.41×, and HEALPix has 63° creases across its polar faces where the smooth cube has none.
  • Decided (2026-10-04): the surface solvers follow the world ("Follow the world decision"): on Lambert's raster while the world is Lambert, on the cube faces if the world is the cube, resampled at the bake for a 2:1 world; rain shadow uses Smith & Barstad plus an upwind moisture budget.

Recommendation

Recommended The Moser equal-area cube, cross net, faces of 3,776 blocks (7,552 at 32k)

Place the eight corners at sea where the planet allows (the bake can search λ₀), keep a small no-build zone round each, and let the engine's band show one face there. Petroff–Guyou is the fallback if corner consistency or portal count is weighed above shape.

The fair case for Petroff–Guyou

  • Corners render consistently: one raw copy is right for every viewer, so the band, the generator's outside, vanilla maps and Distant Horizons need no corner rule; blocks are locally seamless up to the corner.
  • Half the corners, and easier to hide: all four at sea for of turns against .
  • Four portals instead of fourteen, 58 % of the turning seam, no untested quarter-turn portals.
  • Toroidal World keeps the whole east–west wrap and a third of all seam length.
  • F3's north is within 15° of true north on more of the planet ( against ) and a vanilla compass points away from home less often.
  • The plane-covering client route stays possible.
  • Cheapest: 1–2 days of projection work on the prototype that already runs.

The fair case for the Moser cube

  • About half the shape distortion everywhere: of this planet's land stretched over 1.5× against ; continents look like themselves.
  • Both poles are ordinary points mid-face; the polar bands are whole and centred.
  • No creases; the equator is 15 % longer, and walking distances stay close to the planet's own (median × against ×; on Guyou of places are more than 15 % nearer than they should be, on the cube ).
  • Its faces are the simulation's climate grid, and the surface solvers move onto them (Ben's "follow the world"): the bake becomes a copy, not a resample.
  • Mild corners: at most 1.42× stretch round one, three quarters of a normal ring.
  • The engine already has the layout, the quarter-turn GameTests and the Create quarter-turn adapter.

What this page found that changes or sharpens the picture

  1. Cube corners tear; Guyou corners do not. Traced views, the band, the generator's outside and DH all show a tear at each cube corner; Guyou's corners show everything twice but consistently. The survey treated cube corners as purely milder; for rendering and building they are harder. This narrows the cube's lead and adds a small corner keep-out to its cost.
  2. Toroidal World keeps the band's slide on the cross net (cubeCross(…, slides = false)); the "owns no edge" concern applies to the 3 × 2 net only.
  3. Guyou at today's scale is not chunk-aligned in the engine's centred layout (b = 6,544 is not a multiple of 32): use 6,528 or 6,560.
  4. A boundary bug in Petroff's quadrant test put points exactly on two of each square's diagonals in the wrong place; fixed in the survey's port. The survey's numbers do not depend on it.

The cube's own sub-choices

  • Net: cross (assumed above) or 3 × 2. The 3 × 2 rectangle packs the raster without waste but has 8 seam pairs, six seam edges on the line between its rows and no world-wide slide for Toroidal World.
  • Face size: 3,776 (assumed above) or 4,096. Powers of two put every seam on a region line and make the band exactly 16,384 round (today's wrap), at 1.18× the blocks for the same planet.
  • Face map: the smooth Moser table (assumed above) or closed-form QSC, which folds every ridge 41° on each face diagonal.

Method and sources

Pictures and numbers. tools/review/world-shape/compute.py on the test VM (run.sh, through the admission tool) builds both worlds on the survey's code (tools/research/projection-survey/: Petroff's inverse, the conformal cube by Schwarz triangle maps made equal-area by Moser's flow at 512²) and resamples deeptime heightmap of the bake onto them. Walks step through the seam motions. Views and walking distances are rays traced through the seams (4,000–4,320 rays, 4–10-block steps). One raw copy round a corner uses the engine's edge tables in WrapLayouts order, re-implemented in Python and checked against the survey's seams to rad (cube) and rad (Guyou). Tears are neighbouring pixels more than six pixel widths apart on the planet. Every seam closes to rad (Guyou) and rad (cube). Block grids use the engine's cell motions (CellIsometry) at production sizes.
