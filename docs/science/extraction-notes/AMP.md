## Simplifications and game choices

- **Iteration counts stand in for time.** Erosion, thermal and deposition passes (40 / 24 / 12) are numbers of Jacobi passes, not years; they replace Schott's hundreds to thousands because each level starts from the coarse level's exact drainage area (AMP-002 to AMP-004, AMP-007). Reason given: speed and tiling ("a few dozen iterations replace their hundreds").
- **Every pass is a pure function of a bounded neighbourhood** so a tiled run equals a whole-raster run bit for bit; the flood is the one non-local step and relies on `repair_margin_cells` (32).
- **Incision is capped as a multiple of the cell's own drop** (AMP-031, AMP-033): valley depth scales with cell size, not with river power or time; the cap is Ben's "Terrain carving" setting.
- **Deposition mass is not kept past the pass's reach** (about half of the eroded volume is reported "in transit" and leaves; "no mass claim beyond the pass"), build log P1-06 finding 5.
- **The final flood fills every pit** so the surface has no new sinks (AMP-017), at the price of filling overdeepened glacial basins flat (GLA-051: "lake floors until water lands in Phase 2"; the build log says so).
- **Resampling clamps Catmull-Rom to the two nearest samples** (AMP-067): no coastal rims, but flat-topped peaks that the passes must give back; the audit measures creases at the coarse points.
- **Multiple flow on a square grid** gives gullies that "still run mostly along the grid's axes and 45 degrees" (build log P1-06, "Doubtful"); the audit lists D8 river straightness as open.
- **The mountain character is a procedural detail layer, not erosion**: stripe-noise gullies aligned with slope, ridged fold stripes along the plate model's fold trend, unsharp-mask young peaks, terraced cliffs on hard rock. It reads the simulation's fields (orogeny age, uplift, fold trend, erodibility) but every coefficient is a tuned design number (AMP-040 to AMP-066). Reason: Ben, decisions log 2026-09-29, wanted block-scale mountain shape "driven by the simulation" in Phase 1. Talus and scree at cliff feet and 3D crags and overhangs are deferred (surface-block rule the column evaluator would have to mirror; Phase 2).
- **Drama** (0 to 2) is Ben's slider; its mapping 0.25 + 0.75 * drama is the agent's so that 0 is smooth, not flat.
- **Glacial model**: a diagnostic steady-state ice solve per substep (flux routed down the fluvial D8 tree, ice surface by bisection on the shallow-ice law), not a time-evolving ice dynamics model; the research note records that goSPL removed its own SIA solve because it diverged at long steps (here the solve is local and per substep, four substeps per step). Glacier widths are `H / 0.3` (a "design value"), at most seven cells; erosion is spread parabolically over the width. Accumulation and ablation caps (2 and 10 m/yr), `Q_MIN`, `max_erosion_share`, `max_till_m` are implementation limits.
- **Glacial valley width 3** (Ben, 2026-09-30): the young landscape draws glacier footprints and their erosion three times wider than physical so U-valleys do not read as gorges at 15x vertical exaggeration; flow law, thickness and eroded volume unchanged; the deep-time surface stays physical.
- **Glacial strength kept at 1 "physical"** (Ben, 2026-09-30) although the deep-time glaciers lowered the p99 height by 1.3 km on the first seed; heights to be rechecked in P1-17.
- **Block-scale glacial shaping** (amplify/glacial.rs) redistributes rock: the carved volume is partly (50 %) returned to nearby ice-free crests, capped at 150 m, so the coarse level's mean height is kept and ridges sharpen into arretes; this is a design device justified in the module doc by the glacial buzzsaw.
- **Not modelled**: eskers, drumlins, a crisp moraine crest line; hanging valleys and cirques only emerge from the physics and the parabola.
- **Young landscape keeps fluvial erodibility under the glacial state's ice** (GLA-035), because removing it lifted lowlands by about 200 m.
- **Climate coupling is partial**: the glacial ELA is today's minus 500 m (Porter's average Quaternary snowline) while the young landscape's allowed area is the last-glacial-maximum extent (a colder state than the average); deep-time ice uses the climate's own ice fields.

## Flags

Admissions and suspicions. "Suspicion" means my reading of the code and text, not a check against a source.

1. **Ohmura, Kasser & Funk 1992 recalled, not re-read.** STATUS.md 2026-10-01 "P1-22b follow-up: cold-climate runoff reviewed": "close to the summer temperature at the equilibrium line that Ohmura et al. 1992 give for so little accumulation, about -2 degC; cited from memory, not re-read". The same paper's polynomial drives GLA-037 and every ELA (GLA-031, GLA-036); the P1-05 entries and glaciers.rs name the paper without saying how it was read. The EF entry (2026-09-29) adds that the fit "is extrapolated below its data".
2. **Stale comments against the coded default, flow exponent.** `amplify/kernels.rs` module doc says "their `flow_p` 1.3; here 1.5 by default, `s*sqrt(s)`"; `AmplifyParams.flow_power` is 2.0 and its comment says "(Schott: 1.3)"; the build log says "flow exponent 2 (1.5 combed the gullies)". The coded 2.0 is a tuned value (AMP-006); the 1.5 in kernels.rs is out of date. `pw()` in kernels.rs still has a 1.5 fast path.
3. **Coarse-level iteration factor contradicts the docs.** `AmplifyParams.coarse_iteration_factor` default is 2.0, but STATUS.md 2026-09-29 P1-06 and plan section 8.2 both say "40 iterations (x3 on the levels before the last)". The module doc of amplify.rs says "coarse_iteration_factor x the iterations" without a number (AMP-005).
4. **Thermal slope attribution.** The `thermal_slope` comment says "Schott: tan 30 deg = 0.57", the default is 0.6. The research note (surface_processes_climate.md) attributes the critical slope atan 30 deg (0.57) to Tzathas 2024, and records only that Schott 2024 "includes a thermal pass"; no document I read quotes Schott's own angle (AMP-034). Schott's noise multiplier "x0.9 to 1.4" (thermal.rs) differs from the coded plus or minus 25 % (AMP-038), also deliberately.
5. **Slope cap differs from the paper's.** `slope_cap` 0.5 against Schott's `min(S, 1)` (AMP-028); the comment mentions the Schott form but not that the value changed.
6. **Aletsch example does not match the design ratio.** surface/glacial.rs says the valley glacier is `1 / form_ratio` as wide as thick ("a design value of 0.3; Aletsch Glacier is about 900 m thick and about 1.5 km wide"). 900/1,500 = 0.6, twice 0.3 (GLA-022). Hergarten 2021 is cited for thickness proportional to width, with no number.
7. **Cited relief limit unused.** amplify/glacial.rs cites Mitchell & Humphries 2015 "relief above cirque floors rarely over 600 m"; the coded `max_ridge_raise_m` is 150 m with no stated derivation (GLA-062).
8. **Glacial rates against the calibration target.** The comment says K_g = 1e-5 gives "fast outlet glaciers 10-100 mm/yr" (Hallet et al.), which at l = 1 needs sliding of 1,000 to 10,000 m/yr; the measured maximum glacial erosion of any cell in the two runs is 1,351 and 1,121 m/Myr (about 1.1 to 1.4 mm/yr). No log entry reconciles these (the cell-mean over a step on a 16 to 8 block grid may dilute the rate). Suspicion only.
9. **Sliding law stress.** With the coded B = 30 m/yr per (100 kPa)^3 the comment's "sliding 100-200 m/yr" corresponds to basal shear stresses of about 150 to 190 kPa (30 * (tau/100 kPa)^3); the file says nothing about the stress a temperate valley glacier carries. Suspicion only; arithmetic from the code, no outside check.
10. **ELA rule mixing.** The young landscape lowers today's ELA by 500 m (average Quaternary, Porter 1989; the same comment gives about 900 m at the last glacial maximum) yet allows glaciers only inside the last-glacial-maximum extent (GLA-030, GLA-033). Not wrong by itself; the two numbers describe different climates.
11. **Build log versus code, fold strength.** Build log (mountains) says Andean 0.4 "x youth"; the code multiplies by `0.3 + 0.7 * youth_age` (AMP-051), so old orogens keep 30 % of the strength. Build log says cliffs where erodibility is "at most about 1.3 and slope over 0.6"; the code is smooth ramps (hardness from 0.45 to 0.9, slope from 0.6 to 2.0), so full cliffs need erodibility near 1.1 and slope near 2 (AMP-059).
12. **Three roundings of one number.** T_ELA at zero precipitation is written -2.4 (glaciers.rs module doc), -2.35 (its test) and -2.3 (EF build log entry): the same value from the coded formula; trivial but inconsistent.
13. **Real-river sinuosity "from memory, not sourced".** grid-artifact-audit.md, finding 2: "Real rivers are commonly 1.1-1.5 and over (from memory, not sourced)", used to say the amplifier's rivers (median 1.035) are too straight.
14. **Earth summit relief "Earth: 5 km + 3-4 km"** (STATUS 2026-10-01 height stage 2 design) has no source.
15. **Decisions log date.** `amplify.rs` cites the ruggedness setting as "decisions log 2026-09-29"; the default was confirmed 2026-09-30 ("Keep Natural (1)").
16. **Preset comparison.** In `earthlike_quick_16k.json` every amplify and glacial key present equals the Rust default (incision_cap 3.0, ruggedness 1.0, amplify.glacial strength 1.0 and ridge_share 0.5, surface.glacial strength 1.0, young_ela_drop_m 500.0, valley_width 3.0, surface.drama 1.0). Outside my scope but seen in the same file: surface.k_spl is 3e-09 in the preset against 2.0e-9 in the Rust default (`surface/params.rs`) and the module doc of the same file.
17. **Amplifier sink count.** `notes.amplify.sinks` is asserted 0 by tests; the glacial entry reports the final flood raising 22,330 cells up to 121 m (267 m with the deep-time climate): the "no new sinks" guarantee is bought with flat basin floors, not with a physical lake model.
18. **Not covered by anyone I know of**: `surface/detail.rs` (the detail layer the amplifier writes: amplitude = min(drama * 0.12 * relief5, 6 * drama) blocks damped along channels, per the P1-06 entry, and its noise constants; not read here), `params::BuildFit` (the build-limit compression applied in `amplify/tiles.rs: PackSink`).
