## Simplifications and game choices

- **Widths and depths are the block world's, not Earth's.** Plan §8.4: "the coefficients are the block world's, not the research's, and are settings"; the design table row "Local features" enlarges rivers to playable size with a per-feature multiplier. The size of the enlargement is not stated anywhere.
- **No deltas, floodplains (HAND), meanders beyond Gaussian smoothing, or knickpoint waterfalls** (P1-22 entry: "For P2-03"). Steps in the water surface become vanilla's flowing and falling water, "the cheap waterfalls".
- **No incision burned into `elevation`.** Plan §8.4: the channel is cut by the game from a per-column rule "because a 1–2 block stream cannot survive the density's 4-block noise-cell interpolation".
- **Discharge is the annual mean** of runoff accumulated down D8 receivers; no seasonality, groundwater, storage or channel loss.
- **Lakes are an equilibrium per basin**, not a time-dependent volume budget (plan §8.4: "not its volume bookkeeping"). A terminal lake stands where the basin's hypsometry makes losses equal inflow; a lake inside is merged when it is covered. Nesting deeper than 8 passes water on as filled ground.
- **Dry land below sea level is deferred** (Ben, decisions log 2026-10-01): a terminal basin floored a block or more under sea level stands at sea level.
- **Land ice is not read by the hydrology** (build log 2026-10-01 follow-up: "precipitation on an ice sheet runs off as if on bare ground where the summer thaws, and a basin the ice sheet would flow into is still a lake"). Ben agreed to join it with the ice-sheet branch (decisions log 2026-10-01, "Polar lake verdict"). The views hide water under 60 m of ice; the bake and statistics do not.
- **Lake ice is a single rule**: mean annual temperature below 0 degC; one block of ice on the top water block; no thickness or season. The bake writes no ice layer: the game decides from `climate/t_mean_c` (Ben, decisions log 2026-10-01).
- **Salt flats are a 2-block halite crust** on the floor of a too-dry terminal basin; no brine lakes or salt-lake chemistry.
- **Hydrology grid**: the L3 terrain (2 blocks, 600 m a cell at 300 m a block) on every preset but 32k full, averaged 2 × 2 above 40 million cells; the workbench views average to at most 600,000 cells.
- **D8 routing on a square grid** gives eight-direction bias; the audit (finding 2) lists it as open and offers meandering or a stochastic receiver as options.
- **Vertical exaggeration**: 300 m a block across, 35 m up (SCL-002; 20 m up when these notes were taken, "the world is 15× taller than wide", STATUS.md 2026-09-30 volcano entry; today heights are drawn 8.6 times larger than widths). A one-block minimum lake depth is 35 m (20 m then), the one-block freeboard is 35 m, a one-block channel is 300 m wide.
- **Young landscape**: L2 (2.4 km a cell), 4 steps of 0.5 Myr, clock not advanced, uplift equal to the last deep-time erosion rate ("balance"), rivers keep their K under ice. The coastline keeps the L2 staircase (STATUS.md P1-06, "Doubtful").
- **Detail noise** is a cosmetic sub-grid roughness (value noise, three octaves sharing one lattice, amplitude 12 % of local relief capped at 6 blocks, faded out along channels); it carries no physical model and the audit (finding 9) calls its lattice an open, low-priority artefact.
- **Water engineering** (not claims, listed so nobody looks for them): caves under beds plugged up to 8 and 3 blocks, the 17-block margin around sea level for the pass, tile size 32, and the shore rule's 4-block reach.

## Flags

1. **The known flag: sinuosity from memory.** `docs/research/notes/grid-artifact-audit.md`, finding 2: "Sinuosity over 128 blocks of path: 10th percentile 1.010, median 1.035, 90th 1.112; 27 % below 1.02. Real rivers are commonly 1.1–1.5 and over (from memory, not sourced)." And under "Not verified": "Earth figures for river sinuosity are from memory." The STATUS.md summary of the same audit (2026-10-01 P1-23) gives "median sinuosity 1.035" without the Earth figure. The mod figure was measured on one planet; the Earth figure has no source.
2. **Leopold & Maddock exponents are cited, but no sourced hydraulic-geometry values were gathered.** `hydro/mod.rs` cites "Leopold & Maddock 1953: width ∝ Q^0.5, depth ∝ Q^0.4"; `surface_processes_climate.md` §6 Gaps: "No sourced hydraulic-geometry coefficients (width and depth versus discharge) were gathered"; plan §8.4: "coefficients to be sourced in P2 — the research found none". Nothing says the 1953 paper was read. Suspicion (from my own memory, unverified): the 0.5 and 0.4 look like the paper's downstream, mean-annual-flow exponents, so they may be right; the open question is that the code applies them to the model's own discharge with block-world coefficients, and plan §8.4's "(coefficients to be sourced in P2)" is still unmet.
3. **Channel size against the preset's own scale (arithmetic, not a judgement of Earth).** At 300 m a block, `width = 1 · Q^0.5` blocks gives 424 m at 2 m3/s, 3 km at 100 m3/s and about 11 km at the run's largest discharge (1,345 m3/s, widest 36.7 blocks). Depth `0.5 · Q^0.4` blocks at 20 m a block gives about 63 m at 100 m3/s and about 182 m at 1,345 m3/s; the 12-block cap is 240 m. The Leopold & Maddock relation is for real-scale channels; the enlargement factor is not written down.
4. **Editor hint contradicts the preset's scale.** `crates/deeptime-viewer/assets/editor_fields.json`, `lake_min_area`: "The default is 1 km², a pond about 10 × 10 blocks." At the preset's 300 m a block, 1 km² is 11.1 blocks² (about 3.3 × 3.3 blocks); on the 2-block L3 grid the minimum is 3 cells (1.08 km²). 10 × 10 blocks would hold with 100 m a block, which no preset uses (all four have `m_per_block_h` 300).
5. **Comment contradicts comment in the same file.** `hydro/mod.rs` module doc, "Frozen lakes": "in the game it lies in the frozen biomes, whose `freeze_top_layer` ices it". The `lake_freeze_mean_c` doc in the same file says the game "ices the lake's top block there and keeps it open elsewhere, whatever the biome", and the Java (`WaterBodies`, `LakeFreeze`, `LakeIce`) does the second. The module doc is stale. STATUS.md 2026-10-01 "P1-22b lakes sized by the climate" says "The bake marks nothing" and "no Java changed"; the follow-up entry says it "supersedes the one below where they differ".
6. **Quoted decisions-log text that is not in the log.** `PlayaPass.java` quotes "Playas / salt flats as a real surface in the game" and `LakeFreeze.java` quotes "regardless of biome" as from `docs/deeptime-design-decisions.md` log 2026-10-01. Neither phrase is in the file (grep found only "salt flats as a game surface" and "baked lake ice" in the 2026-10-01 "Defer the lakes agent's four smaller items?" row, and the "Both: freeze them and size them by climate" row).
7. **Two different runoff laws are still in the code.** The 2026-10-01 fix replaced the land's PET with biotemperature in the hydrology (HYD-035) because "a land with a mean below freezing evaporated nothing". `surface/young.rs` (weights, `e = (60.0 * tc).max(0.0)`) and `surface/climate.rs` `snapshot` (`let e = (60.0 * tc).max(0.0)`) still use the old law, so the young stage's runoff weights and the surface stage's runoff differ from the hydrology's for cold land. Also `Climate::aridity` and `young.rs` use the constant 60, not `HydroParams::pet_mm_per_c`.
8. **Two stories for the same 60 mm/degC.** `surface/climate.rs`: "PET = 60 mm/°C · T … a Thornthwaite-like linear stand-in"; `hydro/mod.rs`: Holdridge's 58.93, "rounded to the surface stage's 60". The stand-in is uncited; Holdridge is cited but not shown to have been read. Suspicion (from my own memory, unverified): Holdridge's biotemperature also zeroes temperatures above 30 degC; `Climate::thaw` only zeroes below 0 and the doc says nothing of an upper limit.
9. **Earth lake-share comparison is ambiguous.** The run review compares against 3.7 % (Verpoorter, lakes down to 0.002 km2); the build log gives both 1.8 % (lakes of 10 ha and more) and 3.7 %. The mod's minimum lake is 1 km2 (and 20 m deep), between the two Earth cutoffs, so neither figure is like-for-like. After the cold-climate fix the mod reads 1.42–1.49 % on two planets, under both.
10. **Lake counts changed by two orders of magnitude between builds with no explanation in the entries read.** P1-22 (2026-09-30): 27 lakes, 68,139 km2 (the first build on master e5d98e3 gave 26); P1-22b and the audit (2026-10-01): 931–1,224 lakes for similar lake areas (62,398 km2 on Bake A). The entries attribute later differences to the terrain of a newer master but do not say why the count rose ~40 times.
11. **Open-sea threshold differs by stage.** Hydrology: 5,000 km2 (OPEN_SEA_KM2). Young stage: at least n/500 cells, 4,194 cells of 5.76 km2 = about 24,000 km2 on the 16k L2 grid (arithmetic from `young.rs`). A body between the two is a lake to one and sea to the other.
12. **`water_view` "about nine cells".** `water_view.rs` says "on a 4.8 km cell a stream starts where about nine cells drain into it". The area threshold (50 km2) alone is 2.2 cells of 23.04 km2; nine cells (207 km2) fits only if runoff is about 300 mm a year (2 m3/s = 63 million m3 a year). The assumption is unstated.
13. **Calibration rests on an unsourced assumption.** HYD-040's "200 + 60 · 7 meets" 650 mm uses "about 6–8 °C of mean temperature above freezing" for Earth's lake area in Canada, Siberia and Scandinavia, with no source; the result is 620 mm, not 650. One-point calibration; no mod-wide mean lake evaporation is reported to check it. The Zhao figures (1,500 ± 150 km3, 2.3 million km2) are given as 650 mm: 1,500 / 2.3 million = 0.652 m (consistent).
14. **"Polar lakes lose 150–350 mm" has no source** and decides a close case: the build log says the 200 mm base "decides" the polar lake ("a close call that the default decides for 'full'"); the lake would stop filling at about 260 mm.
15. **Ohmura check from memory.** STATUS.md 2026-10-01 follow-up: "close to the summer temperature at the equilibrium line that Ohmura et al. 1992 give for so little accumulation, about −2 °C; cited from memory, not re-read". The rule's −1 degC against −2 degC is called "close".
16. **The lake-sediment dams and straight rivers are open design problems** (grid audit findings 1 and 2, state "open, design"): lake shores within 2° of an axis 7.3 % (isotropic 4.4 %); the dams are 3–8 blocks high and 150 to over 1,000 blocks long. The cause is "inferred from `surface/spl.rs`", not proven by a run with the rule changed (Not verified section).
17. **Young lake share vs hydrology lake share.** The audit says the young landscape "has 34,988 lake cells, 6 % of the land" while the hydrology's lakes on the same kind of planet are 1.4–2.5 % of land. The young stage counts any depression deeper than LAKE_TOL_M (0.01 m) as a lake cell; the hydrology needs 1 km2 and a block (20 m) of depth. Different definitions, not reconciled in the text.
18. **Uplift "balance" assumption has no recorded check.** YNG-003: the young stage assumes uplift equals the last deep-time step's erosion rate; no target or comparison for it is written down in the files read. The `young.rs` comment says that taking the river K away under ice "lifted whole lowlands by the uplift of 2 Myr in the first runs", i.e. the result is sensitive to it.
19. **Editor hint for snow melt**: "real snow melts 3 to 8" mm per degC per day has no source; the default 4 is inside it. Hint "a 100 m3/s river 10 blocks wide … about 3 blocks deep" is arithmetic-consistent with the coefficients (1.0 · 100^0.5 = 10; 0.5 · 100^0.4 = 3.16).
20. **Not run or not covered in this extraction**: `crates/deeptime-core/src/hydro/tests.rs` and the other test files were not read; the amplifier (`surface/amplify.rs`, `amplify/*`, `thermal.rs`) and `deeptime_format::detail::Noise` internals (hash constants) are outside this scope; `surface/glacial.rs` and the surface-stage physics the young stage reuses belong to other ledgers.
