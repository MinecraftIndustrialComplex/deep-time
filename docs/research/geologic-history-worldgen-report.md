# Build the planet, borrow the bridge

**Ben cannot embed any real geoscience engine in a NeoForge 1.21.1 mod, but he does not have to invent one either: three published models and a handful of MIT-licensed codes cover the whole simulation, and an MIT-licensed 1.21.1 mod already solves most of the Minecraft bridge.** GPlates/pyGPlates, fastscapelib, goSPL and the climate models are GPL, HPC-bound or both, and pyGPlates only replays plate motions a human supplies, so the engine has to be a pure-Java port of Cortial et al.'s 2019 procedural plate model (about 1.3–1.8 minutes per 500 Myr at 78 km resolution on a 2016 desktop), the Braun–Willett implicit stream-power method (about 1 µs per cell per step), the Noddy kinematic evaluator for block-level folds, faults and intrusions (MIT-licensed C), and a mineral-systems ore engine sized from public-domain USGS grade–tonnage data. Into Minecraft, the right path is to subclass `NoiseBasedChunkGenerator` and feed its noise router from simulation rasters, porting the plumbing from ReTerraForged 1.21.1 (MIT). That route keeps Terralith's biomes and surface rules, TerraBlender biome mods, structures, caves, C2ME and Distant Horizons working, where offline baking and custom generators silently lose them. The premise survives review: no mod or shipped game derives ore from simulated geologic history, TerraForged's ~11.7 million downloads have no modern home, and 30,000-block "realistic" survival maps already circulate. But players reward spectacle and legible clues, not realism: Terralith's 23.3 million Modrinth downloads are about 1,060 times Geolosys's 21,911. The decision that shapes everything else is scale. An isotropic 8 m per block in an overworld raised to Tectonic's y640 ceiling fits 4.6 km of relief without distortion, turns a 16,384-block world into a 131 km window where the landforms watched in the timelapse are recognisable, and raises the real porphyry-copper count from 0.04 to about 2.6 deposits on a fully permissive map. The plan starts with a bridge spike that proves Terralith and TerraBlender survive, then a vertical-slice MVP: plates, erosion and climate into terrain and biomes, plus one porphyry-copper story that ends at a Create Ore Excavation drill.

## Nothing scientific embeds, but three published models carry the engine

The geoscience community's real tools answer a different question from Ben's, and none can ride inside a mod. **pyGPlates 1.0.0 is now pip-installable, but it is a reconstruction engine: it moves the plates you give it along the rotations you give it, while deciding where plates go, when ridges and trenches appear and how collisions suture is left to the user** ([GPlates](https://github.com/GPlates/GPlates); [pyGPlates docs](https://www.gplates.org/docs/pygplates/)). Worldbuilders who use it for fictional planets hand-edit rotation files in 10–50 Myr steps and hand-paint elevations afterwards, calling the result "more art than science" ([Worldbuilding Pasta V](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/06/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-v.html); [Part VIIc](https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2023/03/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-viic.html)). It is GPL-2.0-only and about 239 MB installed with native GL and glib dependencies ([pygplates on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pygplates/)). Forward mantle-convection codes produce Earth-like plates only on supercomputers: Coltice et al.'s StagYY run took **nine months and 390,200 time steps** for 1.5 Gyr ([Coltice et al. 2019](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6821462)). The best landscape-evolution codes are GPL-3.0, and goSPL cannot start a 10 km global mesh below about 16 MPI ranks ([goSPL scaling](https://github.com/Geodels/gospl/blob/HEAD/docs/user_guide/hpc_scaling.rst)); the fastest graphics-research erosion codes from Inria are licensed for non-commercial research only ([analytical-terrains licence](https://gitlab.inria.fr/landscapes/analytical-terrains)); and a laptop climate model at 3.75 minutes per simulated year with 50–200 years of spin-up needs 3–12 hours per equilibrium climate ([ExoPlaSim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07685)). **"Real scientific tooling" in this mod therefore means peer-reviewed algorithms re-implemented in Java and validated offline against those tools, not the tools running in-game.** That distinction should also shape how the mod is described to players.

For plate tectonics the blueprint is **Cortial, Peytavie, Galin and Guérin's 2019 "Procedural Tectonic Planets"**, the most complete and geologically reviewed procedural model found. It samples crust on a Fibonacci sphere; each sample carries crust type, thickness, elevation, oceanic age, ridge direction, orogeny type and age, and fold direction. It runs subduction (the older oceanic plate always sinks), collision with terrane transfer once interpenetration exceeds 300 km, Poisson-triggered rifting into 2–4 plates, and new crust at ridges, and its appendix lists every constant: 2 Myr steps, a 6,370 km radius, a 100 mm/yr speed cap ([Cortial et al. 2019](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/eric.galin/Articles/2019-planets.pdf)). A regular step costs 0.28 s at 100,000 samples (78 km) on a 2016 i7-6700K. A 500 Myr run is 250 steps (250 × 0.28 s ≈ 70 s) plus periodic rifting and crust generation, **so about 1.3–1.8 minutes at 78 km and 9–17 minutes at 35 km**, which suits a watch-it-form timelapse. The model won a two-alternative user study 97 votes to 47 against fractal terrain and was reviewed by a tectonics specialist. It has no licensed code: the three GitHub re-implementations carry no licence, which means no permission to reuse ([choosealicense](https://choosealicense.com/no-permission/)). The only maintained embeddable engine, PlaTec (LGPL), ran a 512² world in 10.3 s in the research session but is flat, unitless and power-of-two, and outputs only height, plate ID and crust age ([plate-tectonics](https://github.com/Mindwerks/plate-tectonics); [platecapi.hpp](https://github.com/Mindwerks/plate-tectonics/blob/main/src/platecapi.hpp)). That makes it a throwaway raster source for a prototype, not a geology engine. For rock bookkeeping, tectonics.js keeps seven rock-mass pools per cell under CC BY 4.0 and can be ported with attribution; its successor tectonics.cpp is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and must stay reading material ([RockColumn.js](https://github.com/davidson16807/tectonics.js/blob/master/noncompiled/models/lithosphere/RockColumn.js); [tectonics.cpp](https://github.com/davidson16807/tectonics.cpp)). The heavy geodynamics codes still earn a role as calibration targets: a plate-size split near 10^7.6 km² and ocean crust mostly younger than about 250 Myr ([Mallard et al. 2016](https://web.gps.caltech.edu/~jstock/161/PapersToRead/nature17992-Mallard.pdf); [tectonics.js v2](https://github.com/davidson16807/tectonics.js/blob/master/_posts/2017-9-16-version2.md)).

Plate models stop at 2D fields or coarse columns; **no existing engine produces block-scale 3D lithology**, so the step from plate history to rock at (x, y, z) has to be built. The right method is **Noddy's kinematic evaluation**. An ordered event history (stratigraphy, tilts, folds, faults, unconformities, dykes, plugs, shear zones) is evaluated at any point by unravelling the displacement equations in reverse chronological order, so every point is independent and chunk-lazy ([Jessell et al. 2022](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/14/381/2022/); [Wellmann et al. 2016](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1019/2016/)). Noddy built 200³-voxel models, geophysics included, in under 15 s on one Xeon Gold 6254, and its C source is MIT-licensed ([noddy_qt](https://github.com/Loop3D/noddy_qt); [noddyverse](https://github.com/Loop3D/noddyverse)). The inverse-kinematics core is compact: in the Noddyverse command-line source, `unEvents.c` holds `unfold`, `unfault`, `undyke`, `unplug`, `unbury` and `reverseEvents` in 1,226 lines (counted for this report). At the published upper bound of about 1.9 µs per voxel, though, one 16×16×384 column (98,304 blocks) takes up to 0.19 s single-threaded. That is roughly 2.7–9 times a per-chunk budget of 20–70 ms, derived from measured pregeneration rates (4 threads ÷ 183 chunks/s ≈ 22 ms; 10 threads ÷ 137 chunks/s ≈ 73 ms). So the event model should run on a lattice at world creation and be stored as deformed horizon surfaces. Chunk generation then does a per-column lookup costing about 0.1–0.5 ms per chunk ([Nemez benchmark](https://nemez.net/posts/20240414-minecraft-cpu-performance-testing/page-8/); [ishland C2ME benchmark](https://gist.github.com/ishland/9601a033afad4be5b17ffaed859577fd)). Plate events translate into local events mechanically: a collision becomes folds, thrusts, tilt and metamorphism; a rift becomes normal faults, tilted blocks, an unconformity and syn-rift sediment; an arc becomes plutons, dykes and volcanic strata. Implicit modellers such as LoopStructural (about 8 minutes per model) and GemPy fit field observations that a synthetic world does not have, so they stay offline ([Grose et al. 2021](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/14/3915/2021/)).

Surface processes are the most mature stage and the easiest to port from papers. **Braun and Willett's O(n) implicit stream-power method, Yuan et al.'s deposition term, Barnes' priority-flood and implicit hillslope diffusion together cost about 1 µs per cell per step single-threaded**, derived from 556 s for 460 iterations at 1024² ([Tzathas et al. 2024](https://www-sop.inria.fr/reves/Basilic/2024/TGSC24/Analytical_Terrains_EG.pdf); [FastFlow](https://www-sop.inria.fr/reves/Basilic/2024/JKGFC24/FastFlowPG2024_Author_Version.pdf); [Braun & Willett 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/236741975_A_very_efficient_On_implicit_and_parallel_method_to_solve_the_stream_power_equation_governing_fluvial_incision_and_landscape_evolution)). The research notes turn that rate into a budget of 5–15 minutes on a 6–8-core CPU for a 256²–512² deep-time history plus a 1024² young landscape. Tzathas's analytical solver reaches a final landscape in 8 s at 1024², but its code is Inria research-only, so it must be re-derived from the paper. In fastscapelib the stream-power, ADI-diffusion and priority-flood files are 393, 381 and 181 lines; the rest of its 5,549 code lines is generic grid and flow-graph plumbing that a single-raster port does not need (counted for this report). The library itself is GPL-3.0, so it serves only as an offline oracle ([fastscapelib](https://github.com/fastscape-lem/fastscapelib)). Soil and weathering come from MIT Landlab components (exponential soil production, LithoLayers rock stacks, Bosscher–Schlager carbonate growth, submarine diffusion, HAND floodplains, threshold slopes) and from goSPL's documented water-table duricrust scheme, which produces laterite and bauxite caps with relief inversion ([Landlab](https://github.com/landlab/landlab); [goSPL groundwater](https://github.com/Geodels/gospl/blob/HEAD/docs/tech_guide/groundwater.rst)). These map directly to blocks: regolith depth sets dirt and gravel thickness, a weathering front makes saprolite, low relief under a tropical wet–dry climate makes laterite or bauxite caps, and over-steep slopes make scree ([Laterite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laterite)). Karst is the gap: no landscape model surveyed has dissolution, and the usable code is Paris et al.'s MIT cave-network synthesiser ([Karst-Synthesis](https://github.com/aparis69/Karst-Synthesis)). Stratigraphy should be logged per coarse column as (age, thickness, facies, provenance), with facies assigned by rule: coarse fluvial sediment becomes sandstone, offshore fines shale, photic-zone carbonate limestone, closed arid basins evaporite, and subsiding humid swamps coal. The notes flag those facies rules as a proposal, not a published scheme. Hydrology must not run at one block per cell: a 16,384² pass is 268 million cells, about 1.07 GB per float field and about 100 s of depression resolution alone. Rivers and lakes should instead come from a 4–16-block routing grid, MIT Fill–Spill–Merge lake levels and per-chunk vector carving ([Cordonnier et al. 2019](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/7/549/2019/esurf-7-549-2019.pdf); [Fill–Spill–Merge](https://github.com/r-barnes/Barnes2020-FillSpillMerge)).

Climate is the one stage where the real models are simply too slow, so the in-mod climate is parametric. The chain is a zonal energy-balance model with ice-albedo feedback (equations from MIT climlab), circulation belts, a lapse rate, and linear orographic precipitation (Smith–Barstad, whose MIT reference implementation is a 96-line Python module, or the LFPM at about 2.4 stream-power steps' cost). Köppen classification from monthly temperature and precipitation, an equilibrium-line rule for glaciers and a sea-level curve complete it, at about one or two erosion steps' cost per snapshot ([climlab](https://github.com/climlab/climlab); [orographic-precipitation](https://github.com/fastscape-lem/orographic-precipitation); [LFPM](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/15/2063/2022/gmd-15-2063-2022.pdf); [Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)). ExoPlaSim or SpeedyWeather can still run offline to calibrate a small table of climate response to continent layout.

Ore genesis has no engine anywhere to reuse. No published whole-planet forward model and no shipped game derives ore from simulated tectonics; ProcGenesis advertises ore views but documents no algorithm ([ProcGenesis](https://procgenesis.com/)). Scientifically, though, it is the best-documented stage. **The mineral-systems approach treats a deposit as the coincidence of a metal-and-fluid source, an energy driver, a pathway and a trap, followed by preservation, ranked multiplicatively so that one missing process zeroes the score** ([Skirrow et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oregeorev.2019.103011); [Hronsky & Groves 2008](https://doi.org/10.1080/08120090701581356)). That is exactly a rule engine over simulated fields. The notes reduce all deposit classes to about 20 per-cell fields: tectonic regime, magma type and oxidation, crust and keel thickness, convergence rate and slab age, heat flow and metamorphic grade, fault density, basin fill, redox and brine drivers, global clocks, paleolatitude and climate, sea level, and a burial/exhumation ledger. They also supply published thresholds, such as porphyry favourability near 100 km/Myr convergence, ~15° obliquity and 25–70 Myr slab age, or 85% of sediment-hosted base metals lying within 200 km of a thick–thin lithosphere step ([Butterworth et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016tc004289); [Hoggard et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-020-0593-2)). Sizing follows the USGS three-part method with public-domain data: permissive tracts, a Poisson count from deposit density (porphyry copper P50 about 15 per 100,000 km²), then lognormal tonnage and grade. For porphyries the tonnage percentiles are 33, 240 and 1,500 Mt at P90, P50 and P10, which gives σ = (ln 1500 − ln 33)/2.563 ≈ 1.49 ([Singer 1993](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70018261); [Singer et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.2113/gsecongeo.100.3.491); [USGS OFR 2008-1155](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2008/1155/)). Preservation is a ledger: exhumation runs about 158–167 m/Myr for porphyry and epithermal deposits, so a 1 km-deep epithermal system is gone in roughly 6–7 Myr, and eroded parents spawn placer and supergene children ([Kesler & Wilkinson 2006](https://doi.org/10.2113/gsecongeo.101.5.919); [Sillitoe 2005](https://doi.org/10.5382/av100.22)). Secular switches decide what can exist at all: banded iron formation peaks at roughly 2.65–1.85 Ga, while sandstone uranium and coal need land plants ([Bekker et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.2113/gsecongeo.105.3.467); [Cuney 2010](https://doi.org/10.2113/gsecongeo.105.3.553); [Nelsen et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1517943113)). Fictional ores fit the same schema: a registered custom field with a fallback coupled to a real field, sized from a real analogue. Thermodynamic codes (Perple_X, MAGEMin, PHREEQC) are overkill at runtime and useful only for small offline tables such as metamorphic facies. Petroleum maturity is the exception, cheap enough to run in-sim as Lopatin's TTI, where oil generation starts near TTI 15 and peaks near 75 ([Waples 1980](https://doi.org/10.1306/2f9193d2-16ce-11d7-8645000102c1865d)). So the honest answer to "build it all from scratch?" is: the engine yes, the knowledge no. Every stage has a published blueprint with parameters, several have MIT code to port, the ore data is public domain, and the GPL tools become the test suite.

## Subclass the vanilla noise generator and the biome mods come along

### Four ways in, and only one keeps the pack working

There are four ways to get simulated terrain into a 1.21.1 world, and the NeoForge 21.1.249 source settles which one works. **An offline bake of finished chunks** (Amulet- or WorldPainter-style writers) loads through the game's loading pyramid, where every step from structure references through features is a pass-through. Baked chunks get no biome decoration, no modded ore features, no structures and no generation-time spawns, and NeoForge reports them to mods as not new ([ChunkPyramid](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/minecraft/world/level/chunk/status/ChunkPyramid.java); [ChunkSerializer](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/minecraft/world/level/chunk/storage/ChunkSerializer.java)). **A partial bake** at an intermediate status does resume in 1.21.1, but structures are lost unless the writer serialises them, carver fluids follow the dimension's own generator, and un-baked neighbours are generated by that generator, leaving seams ([ChunkGenerationTask](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/minecraft/server/level/ChunkGenerationTask.java)). **A fully custom `ChunkGenerator`** gives total control but re-implements what vanilla supplies. TerraFirmaCraft keeps a dummy `NoiseBasedChunkGenerator` purely to construct carvers, runs its own aquifer and returns an empty `getBaseColumn`. That breaks World Preview's cross-section and sits behind an open Distant Horizons crash report ([TFCChunkGenerator](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/blob/8aa7f96cae0e7b10c63bf5b183f42b14f693c76f/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/TFCChunkGenerator.java); [World Preview TFC](https://modrinth.com/mod/world-preview-terrafirmacraft); [TFC #2764](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/issues/2764)). TFC's world package runs to 25,152 lines of Java (counted for this report), and a custom generator also switches off every TerraBlender and Lithostitched biome mod at once, as shown below. **The fourth route keeps vanilla's generator and changes only its inputs.** `NoiseBasedChunkGenerator` is non-final, its codec holds just a biome source and a settings holder, and `ChunkMap` builds the `RandomState` from whatever `generatorSettings()` returns. A subclass can therefore supply a noise router whose climate and terrain functions read simulation rasters ([NoiseBasedChunkGenerator](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/minecraft/world/level/levelgen/NoiseBasedChunkGenerator.java); [ChunkMap](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/minecraft/server/level/ChunkMap.java)).

ReTerraForged for 1.21.1 proves the fourth route in a shipping mod, and, decisively, **its LICENSE file is MIT** ([ReTerraForged](https://github.com/Campione01/ReterraForged)). It has no custom generator or biome source. It registers density-function types backed by its own heightmap engine through a lazy `Supplier<WorldLookup>`, captures the seed with a `RandomState` mixin, caches per-chunk tiles through a `NoiseChunk` mixin, and ships a preset screen with a live `DynamicTexture` preview ([CellSampler](https://github.com/Campione01/ReterraForged/blob/ba35ca764f4ace341c0692d80cfa12342503a94b/common/src/main/java/raccoonman/reterraforged/world/worldgen/densityfunction/CellSampler.java); [MixinRandomState](https://github.com/Campione01/ReterraForged/blob/ba35ca764f4ace341c0692d80cfa12342503a94b/common/src/main/java/raccoonman/reterraforged/mixin/MixinRandomState.java); [PresetEditorPage](https://github.com/Campione01/ReterraForged/blob/ba35ca764f4ace341c0692d80cfa12342503a94b/common/src/main/java/raccoonman/reterraforged/client/gui/screen/presetconfig/PresetEditorPage.java)). Counted for this report at commit ba35ca7, that integration layer is 1,432 lines of density-function code, 957 lines of mixins (379 of them TerraBlender compatibility) and 2,507 lines of preset-editor UI. It is a portable head start on nearly everything between the simulation and the game. A second 1.21.1 project, TerraScribe, pursues the same idea under MIT, but it is a pre-alpha skeleton of about 2,100 lines whose last commit was 2026-05-10 ([TerraScribe](https://github.com/Sammasr/terrascribe)).

### The generator Ben writes is small

The recommended class is **`GeologicChunkGenerator extends NoiseBasedChunkGenerator`**, with a codec of `{biome_source, settings, sim, vertical}`. Its constructor derives a `NoiseGeneratorSettings` from the pack's own by reference, so Terralith's surface rules and the aquifer settings carry over, and replaces only the 15-field `noise_router`. The replacements are registered density-function types: `geohist:raster` (a bilinear sample of a named 2D layer, wrapped in `flat_cache`), `geohist:surface_relative` (returning (H(x,z) − y)/k for `depth` and the initial density) and a bounds mask. Vanilla's cave functions stay in by registry reference, so caves and aquifers remain vanilla-like ([NoiseRouter](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/minecraft/world/level/levelgen/NoiseRouter.java)). Custom density functions receive neither seed nor level, so they bind lazily to `<world>/geohist/` as ReTerraForged does. They must be pure and thread-safe, because vanilla already calls them from background executors and C2ME's compiler runs unknown types as delegates ([RandomState](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/minecraft/world/level/levelgen/RandomState.java); [C2ME McToAst](https://github.com/RelativityMC/C2ME-fabric/blob/ver/1.21.1/c2me-opts-dfc/src/main/java/com/ishland/c2me/opts/dfc/common/ast/McToAst.java)). A strata pass runs after `buildSurface` and replaces the remaining `default_block` runs with simulated lithology; it must come after the surface rules because they only ever repaint `default_block` ([SurfaceSystem](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/minecraft/world/level/levelgen/SurfaceSystem.java)). One deposits feature at `UNDERGROUND_ORES` reads the deposit registry. The chunk system never consults the world border, so the density functions return ocean beyond the simulated extent, and the generator sets border and spawn once, in NeoForge's cancellable `LevelEvent.CreateSpawnPosition` ([ServerChunkCache](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/minecraft/server/level/ServerChunkCache.java); [LevelEvent](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/neoforged/neoforge/event/level/LevelEvent.java)). A custom dimension type can raise the build range to as much as 4,064 blocks ([DimensionType](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/minecraft/world/level/dimension/DimensionType.java)). The MIC jars add two traps. Name the derived settings something like `geohist:geologic`, because Tectonic 3.0.26's `NoiseBasedChunkGeneratorMixin` forces its own lava level on any settings key whose path is `overworld`. And test Tectonic's client-side `WorldCreationUiStateMixin`, which drives Tectonic's own preset selector on the same screen the geologic preset editor uses ([Tectonic](https://modrinth.com/mod/tectonic), jar decompiled in the research).

### What the biome libraries key on, and why MIC differs from the public

MIC and the wider ecosystem split into two worldgen families. **MIC runs the Lithostitched family:** Terralith 2.6.2, Tectonic 3.0.26, Lithostitched and Terralith ReStoned are in the pack and TerraBlender is not. Biomes O' Plenty and Oh The Biomes We've Gone hard-depend on TerraBlender's Region API, while Regions Unexplored depends on Lithostitched ([Biomes O' Plenty](https://modrinth.com/mod/biomes-o-plenty); [Lithostitched](https://modrinth.com/mod/lithostitched)). Decompiling TerraBlender 4.1.0.8 shows that it applies to a dimension when three conditions hold. The level stem's `DimensionType` must be in the data tag `terrablender:overworld_regions`, the generator must be `instanceof NoiseBasedChunkGenerator`, and the biome source must be `instanceof MultiNoiseBiomeSource`. It never checks the noise-settings key ([TerraBlender](https://github.com/Glitchfiend/TerraBlender)). Lithostitched scopes its surface-rule and worldgen modifiers by `ResourceKey<LevelStem>` and merges biome lists through the `minecraft:overworld` entry of the parameter-list registry. **The geologic preset must therefore keep the overworld level stem at `minecraft:overworld` and use the shared `multi_noise` source with preset `minecraft:overworld`.** If it raises the build height through its own dimension type, it adds that type to `terrablender:overworld_regions` with one `"replace": false` tag file. Under that design, five of the six mechanisms other mods use survive: new biome parameter points (TerraBlender regions, Lithostitched injectors), features and biome modifiers, structures, separate dimensions, and mixins into the concrete generator and biome-source classes. The Aether and Deeper and Darker are plain `minecraft:noise` dimensions and never touch the overworld generator, and Alex's Caves hooks `MultiNoiseBiomeSource.getNoiseBiome`, which still runs, although its 1.21.1 port was still an unreleased source branch at the research date ([Aether dimension](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/The-Aether-Team/The-Aether/1.21.1-develop/src/generated/resources/data/aether/dimension/the_aether.json); [Otherside dimension](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KyaniteMods/DeeperAndDarker/neoforge-1.21/src/generated/resources/data/deeperdarker/dimension/otherside.json); [Alex's Caves mixin](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AlexModGuy/AlexsCaves/1.21.1/src/main/java/com/github/alexmodguy/alexscaves/mixin/MultiNoiseBiomeSourceMixin.java)). The one mechanism that does not survive is lost on purpose: same-ID `noise_settings` and density-function overrides, which is how Terralith and Tectonic shape terrain, because in geologic worlds the simulation owns terrain. **Terralith still supplies biomes, surface rules, features and structures on simulated land; Tectonic keeps shaping ordinary worlds.**

Biome mods see only six numbers per position, so the router must emit vanilla-range temperature, vegetation, continentalness, erosion, weirdness and depth computed from the simulation. Temperature comes from the energy-balance and lapse-rate model, vegetation from precipitation and aridity, continentalness from land, sea and coast distance, erosion from relief, and weirdness from a ridge–valley index. How touchy this is shows in Tectonic, which ships a hand-merged 325 KB "Terratonic" noise-settings file just to coexist with Terralith (jar contents per the research). One way to keep every biome mod's parameter points in play is rank-preserving mapping; this is a design proposal, not a sourced method. At world creation, the preset editor samples the pack's own router from the frozen registries it is handed (`WorldCreationContext.worldgenLoadContext()`) and maps each simulated field onto that distribution, with continentalness pinned to real coastlines ([WorldCreationContext](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/minecraft/client/gui/screens/worldselection/WorldCreationContext.java)). If Ben later wants biome-mod regions to follow geography (say, one Biomes O' Plenty continent), ReTerraForged's four TerraBlender mixins already redirect `getUniqueness` inside TerraBlender's region lookup to a simulated field and fall back to TerraBlender's own noise underground.

### Floating islands pass through as overlays, not by accident

Terralith's Skylands show what goes wrong if fantasy terrain is ignored. They have two halves kept in sync by hand. The terrain half is a density-function spline that adds up to +1.25 around y≈200–230, but only where continentalness is −0.745 to −0.455, erosion −0.223 to −0.0865, vegetation −0.1 to 0, temperature −0.45 to −0.3 and ridges above 0.1. The biome half is four `terralith:skylands_*` entries in the same continentalness and erosion band ([Terralith](https://modrinth.com/mod/terralith), jar 2.6.2 parsed in the research). The geologic router replaces the terrain half while the biome half keeps firing, **so without a fix, Skylands biome labels would land on open ocean with no islands above them.** The recommended fix is a small overlay registry: `final_density = max(simulated terrain, overlays)`. Each overlay is a density-function holder listed in a datapack tag such as `#geohist:terrain_overlays`, rewired with `DensityFunction.mapAll` so that its references to `overworld/continents`, `erosion`, `vegetation`, `temperature` and `ridges` read the simulation's values. Terralith's islands then appear exactly where its own Skylands biomes land. The Terralith entry ships as a pairwise file gated on `neoforge:mod_loaded`, the same idiom Tectonic uses for its `overlay.terratonic` ([Tectonic](https://modrinth.com/mod/tectonic)); the research found no generic cross-mod mechanism anywhere, so pairwise compatibility is simply the norm. The `mapAll` rewiring is a design inference that the first spike must verify. Floating-island datapacks that overwrite `noise_settings/overworld.json` just do not apply in geologic worlds unless they are listed as overlays ([FloatingIslands-Datapack](https://github.com/Youmiel/FloatingIslands-Datapack)).

### Ores and stones resolve through tags, and MIC's machines need two mixins

The simulation decides lithology and deposit geometry; tags decide blocks. Rock classes map through tags such as `#geohist:rock/granite` to `minecraft:granite`, a Create stone or any stone mod's block, and materials resolve through NeoForge's `c:ores/<material>` convention ([Tags.java](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/neoforged/NeoForge/1.21.1/src/main/java/net/neoforged/neoforge/common/Tags.java)). NeoForge defines host-ground tags only for stone, deepslate and netherrack. Ore-in-schist or ore-in-limestone blocks therefore come from the mod's own per-host blocks (TerraFirmaCraft's model) or from Excavated Variants 4.3.1, which generates missing ore × stone variants at runtime ([Excavated Variants](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lukebemishprojects/ExcavatedVariants/1.21.x/README.md)). One useful side effect follows from the order of generation steps. Vanilla and modded ore features match `#minecraft:stone_ore_replaceables` (stone, granite, diorite, andesite) or `#minecraft:deepslate_ore_replaceables` (deepslate, tuff), and the strata pass runs before features. **An ore the deposit engine does not claim therefore appears only in host rocks its own rule test accepts, with no extra code** ([stone_ore_replaceables](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/1.21.1-data/data/minecraft/tags/block/stone_ore_replaceables.json)). Claimed materials should lose their scatter features through `neoforge:remove_features`, shipped in a built-in datapack enabled only for geologic worlds; that per-world scoping is still to verify. Vanilla's large ore veins are hard-coded copper-in-granite and iron-in-tuff and should be switched off with `ore_veins_enabled: false` ([BiomeModifiers](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/neoforged/neoforge/common/world/BiomeModifiers.java); [OreVeinifier](jar:file:///home/greencheetah/Projects/mic/mods/mic-climate/build/moddev/artifacts/neoforge-21.1.249-sources.jar!/net/minecraft/world/level/levelgen/OreVeinifier.java)). Both of MIC's extraction mods choose a chunk's resource in one static method, which keeps the hooks small; the class and method names below come from `javap` on the pack's own jars for this report. **Create Ore Excavation 1.6.8 uses no worldgen registries at all.** Vein types are `createoreexcavation:vein` recipes placed on a salted spacing/separation grid (copper at spacing 128 and separation 8, diamond at 256 and 64), and vein state is stored per chunk as a NeoForge data attachment ([Create Ore Excavation](https://github.com/tom5454/Create-Ore-Excavation)). The per-chunk choice is `com.tom.createores.OreVeinGenerator.pick(LevelChunk)`, returning a `RecipeHolder<VeinRecipe>`, and the stored `OreData` exposes `setRecipe(ResourceLocation)` and `setRandomMul(float)`. COE's KubeJS classes are recipe schemas (`VeinRecipeJS`, `DrillingRecipeJS`, `ExtractorRecipeJS`), and none of them is a chunk-assignment event. A cancellable HEAD injection into `pick` can return the vein recipe mapped from the simulated deposit under that chunk, with `setRandomMul` scaled by the deposit's tonnage. That pins COE's Vein Finder and drills to real geology. How `randomMul` combines with the `finiteAmountBase` default of 1,000 was not decompiled. **Create: Diesel Generators 1.3.15 computes a chunk's crude in `OilChunksSavedData.getBaseOilAmount(ServerLevel, ChunkPos)`.** If KubeJS is loaded, it first calls `CDGKubeJSPlugin.calculateOilChunks(biomes, chunkPos, seed)` and uses any answer other than −1. Otherwise it samples Perlin noise over chunk coordinates scaled by `OIL_CHUNK_SCALE`, checks the `oil_biomes` and `deny_oil_biomes` tags, and scales a 7,000,000 base by `OIL_MULTIPLIER`, or `HIGH_OIL_MULTIPLIER` in tagged biomes ([Create: Diesel Generators](https://modrinth.com/mod/create-diesel-generators)). A HEAD mixin there, or in MIC a KubeJS handler since KubeJS is in the pack, can return the charge of the simulated trap under that chunk. CDG's Oil Scanner then finds real anticlines and salt domes instead of noise. The same reservoir record can later carry a maturity index for mic_petrochem, so pumped crude could run lighter or gassier where the source rock went deeper into the oil window.

### Runtime cost is a few percent because the heavy work already happened

Chunk generation stays cheap because the expensive work finished before the world opened. Ten 2D layers sampled through `flat_cache` cost about 2.5–12.5 µs per chunk, and the per-column strata pass about 0.1–0.5 ms. Against the 20–70 ms per-chunk budget derived above, that is a few percent. Raster data for a 16,384-block world is about 100–400 MiB raw at 2–16-block resolution. A full pregeneration is 1,048,576 chunks and about 5–30 GiB, taking 1.6 hours at 183 chunks/s and 2.9 hours at 100 chunks/s; an 8,192-block world is a quarter of that ([Region file format](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Region_file_format)). Chunky's `worldborder` mode pregenerates exactly the bordered map, and Distant Horizons' `IDhApiWorldGenerator` can build LODs for the whole map from the rasters without generating chunks ([Chunky world borders](https://github.com/pop4959/Chunky/wiki/World-borders); [DH API](https://distant-horizons-team.gitlab.io/distant-horizons/com/seibel/distanthorizons/api/interfaces/override/worldGenerator/IDhApiWorldGenerator.html)).

## The premise holds if the terrain stays dramatic and the ore stays legible

The ecosystem's numbers point one way: **players adopt spectacular terrain by the tens of millions and geology-as-mechanic by the tens of thousands.** On Modrinth alone, Biomes O' Plenty has 36.4 million downloads, Terralith 23.3 million and Tectonic 16.5 million, all updated within days of the research date. Geolosys has 21,911 and is stalled at 1.20.1, Immersive Geology has 25,459 and GeoCraft 235, and TerraFirmaCraft, the one deep geology overhaul, sits at 719,575 ([Terralith](https://modrinth.com/mod/terralith); [Tectonic](https://modrinth.com/mod/tectonic); [Geolosys](https://modrinth.com/mod/geolosys); [TerraFirmaCraft](https://modrinth.com/mod/terrafirmacraft)). The simulation-flavoured terrain mods prove the other half of the demand. TerraForged (~11.7 million CurseForge downloads) and Realistic Terrain Generation (12.5 million+) are frozen on 1.18.2 and 1.12.2, and the 2026 revival FreeTerraForged has 795 downloads: a vacuum, not a lack of interest ([TerraForged](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/terraforged); [RTG](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/realistic-terrain-generation); [FreeTerraForged](https://modrinth.com/mod/freeterraforged)). Tectonic and Terralith build their "realistic" look from hand-tuned splines rather than simulation, and no shipped game derives ore from simulated geology, so the simulated-history niche is empty.

Outside Minecraft, "simulate the history, then play in it" sells. Dwarf Fortress passed a million Steam copies (VG Insights estimates about $19.9 million gross) on worlds whose history feels as if it "happened for a reason", and WorldBox holds 96% positive across 29,730 reviews ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/04/04/dwarf-fortress-sales-800k-steam-1-million-wishlists); [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-47594); [DF wiki](https://www.dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/World_generation); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1206560/WorldBox__God_Simulator/)). Inside Minecraft, bounded realistic worlds are already a product. Hand-built 30,000- and 40,000-block "massive and realistic" survival maps are listed for servers, EarthMC has run an Earth-map geopolitics server since 2016, and Terradivisa makes capturing ore-bearing territory the only way to get ore ([Terra-Titan](https://www.planetminecraft.com/project/terra-titan-1-20-1-21-massive-and-realistic-30-000-x-30-000-map-survival-friendly-mmorpg-focused-java-edition/); [Terra-Mar](https://www.planetminecraft.com/project/terra-mar-1-20-massive-and-realistic-40-000-x-40-000-map-survival-friendly-mmorpg-focused-java-edition/); [EarthMC](https://earthmc.net/); [TopG listing](https://topg.org/minecraft-servers/type/Geopolitical/)). The "watch it form" pitch has a live audience too: World Preview drew 1,992,325 downloads before going stale in 2024, and two independent successors appeared in 2026 ([World Preview](https://modrinth.com/mod/world-preview); [Genesis](https://modrinth.com/mod/genesis-world-previewer-pregenerator)). Old forum requests for a tectonic-plates mod were answered with "impossible to code"; that objection concerned running tectonics live in infinite worldgen, which an offline history feeding a finite world sidesteps ([Minecraft Forum](https://www.minecraftforum.net/forums/mapping-and-modding-java-edition/minecraft-mods/requests-ideas-for-mods/1328878-idea-minecraft-tectonic-plates-mod-volcanoes-cave)). Education is a smaller but real channel; the British Geological Survey built a GB-geology Minecraft world ([BGS](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/discovering-geology/maps-and-resources/maps/gb-geology-minecraft-world/)).

The counter-evidence is specific. Terrain mods die at version jumps: TerraForged, RTG and Open Terrain Generator all froze, OTG's fork "due to issues with Mojang" ([OTG Continued](https://modrinth.com/mod/open-terrain-generator-continued); [Info Toast](https://infotoast.org/site/index.php/2022/03/13/openterraingenerator-the-death-of-an-11-year-old-minecraft-mod/)). Mojang's 26.3 snapshots already removed `flat_cache`, `cache_2d`, `cache_all_in_cell` and `shifted_noise` and renamed `cache_once`, the very markers this design uses ([Density function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Density_function)). Realistic scarcity turns into tedium without information. TerraFirmaCraft players complain of going "thousands of blocks to find a nickel vein", an Immersive Engineering player "mined … for about an hour and found nothing", and a COE user spent "3 hours straight flying in the nether" ([FTB forum](https://forum.feed-the-beast.com/threads/terrafirmacraft-opinions.51111/); [IE #2229](https://github.com/BluSunrize/ImmersiveEngineering/issues/2229); [COE #85](https://github.com/tom5454/Create-Ore-Excavation/issues/85)). Persisting per-block ore data froze Geolosys servers for about 20 s once its data file passed 50 MB ([Geolosys #158](https://github.com/oitsjustjose/Geolosys/issues/158)). Long generation is not a known deal-breaker: servers already pregenerate for hours with Chunky before opening ([Chunky](https://github.com/pop4959/Chunky/wiki/Pregeneration)).

Two further tensions follow from the physics rather than the sources. **Realism flattens drama if scale is handled badly.** Keeping 1 m per block horizontally while compressing relief k-fold divides slopes by k: a 30° hillside becomes about 16° at k = 2 and 6.6° at k = 5, gentler than the Tectonic terrain players already love. **Realism also narrows biome variety.** One degree of latitude on a 6,370 km-radius planet is 2π × 6,370 / 360 ≈ 111 km, so even a 131 km window spans about 1.2° and has essentially one zonal climate, leaving variety to elevation, rain shadow and coastline. The verdict: the mod makes sense and has no direct competitor, but its public audience will be sized by the beauty of its terrain and the clarity of its prospecting, not by its scientific depth. For MIC the fit is stronger than for the public. Regionally concentrated copper, lead-zinc, uranium and oil give Create trains, airships and COE drills a reason to span a map; it is Terradivisa's territory loop in a Create pack.

## A Minecraft-free core, a world data folder and a thin adapter

The architecture separates what will outlive Minecraft versions from what will not. **The simulation is a Minecraft-free Java 21 library; its only output is a world data folder; a thin NeoForge adapter reads that folder lazily during chunk generation.** The same core jar runs inside the create-world screen for singleplayer and as a command-line tool for servers.

```text
geohist-core  (pure Java 21, no Minecraft classes, MIT)
  PlanetModel    Cortial-style plates on a Fibonacci sphere, 2 Myr steps, per-sample event log
  RegionModel    window landscape: stream power + deposition + diffusion + weathering + strata log
  ClimateModel   energy balance + belts + lapse rate + orographic rain + Koppen + ELA, per snapshot
  StructureModel plate events -> Noddy event list -> horizon surfaces + intrusion bodies
  DepositEngine  mineral-system scores -> USGS counts and sizes -> exhumation ledger -> registry
  Keyframes      immutable frames for the timelapse
        |
        v  writes
<world>/geohist/   tiled rasters, horizons, deposit registry, keyframes, manifest (seed, version)
        |
        v  read lazily, thread-safe
geohist-neoforge  (thin adapter)
  GeologicChunkGenerator extends NoiseBasedChunkGenerator + geohist:raster / surface_relative DFs
  StrataPass after buildSurface | DepositFeature at UNDERGROUND_ORES | CreateSpawnPosition handler
  HistoryScreen (PresetEditor)  | History Atlas item               | CLI entry point
  compat/  terralith overlays | terrablender regions | coe pick() | cdg getBaseOilAmount() | dh LODs
```

Four constraints from the runtime research fix the implementation details. **The authoritative simulation is scalar Java.** Java has had strict IEEE 754 semantics everywhere since JEP 306, so a fixed operation order gives bit-identical results across machines. Vectorised, native or GPU code reorders sums and loses that guarantee ([JEP 306](https://openjdk.org/jeps/306); [arXiv 2408.05148](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.05148)). The Vector API is still an incubator on Java 21 and needs a launch flag a mod cannot set ([JEP 448](https://openjdk.org/jeps/448)). Native solvers crash on virtualised hosts: Create: Power Grid's native backend hit `SIGILL` on a QEMU virtual CPU and ships no macOS binary ([PowerGrid #457](https://github.com/patryk3211/PowerGrid/issues/457)). And macOS stops at OpenGL 4.1, below the 4.3 that compute shaders need, while hosted servers have no GPU at all ([OpenGL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenGL)). **Large state lives off-heap in memory-mapped tiles** (`FileChannel.map`), because launcher defaults are 2–4 GB even though 200-mod packs want 10–16 GB ([Prism defaults](https://help.bisecthosting.com/hc/en-us/articles/50075520613787-How-to-Allocate-More-RAM-in-the-Prism-Launcher); [OuiHeberg](https://www.ouiheberg.com/en/blog/minecraft-modded-server-guide)). `MemorySegment` is avoided because the Foreign Function & Memory API is a preview feature on Java 21 ([JEP 442](https://openjdk.org/jeps/442)), and tiles are compressed with the JDK's own Deflater so that no native compressors are needed. **The deposit registry is sparse and regenerated deterministically**, never persisted per block, which is the Geolosys lesson. **Scales are coupled by functions, not samples.** At 78–102 km per planet sample, a 131 km window spans barely two samples. The window's landscape model is therefore forced by Cortial's uplift terms, which depend on distance to the subduction front, relative speed and elevation, evaluated on the window's fine grid against the planet's boundary lines. That coupling is a design inference that no source has tested.

Several links remain unproven and belong in the first two phases rather than in faith. No end-to-end Cortial-to-Noddy prototype exists anywhere, so its visual credibility at block scale is unknown. No JVM throughput has been measured for these kernels. Nobody has placed river water above sea level through a vanilla-router mod, since vanilla fluids come only from the aquifer. TerraBlender initialisation for a level stem created fresh by a preset was reasoned from source, not observed. And pregeneration throughput inside a 190-mod pack is unmeasured.

## Thirteen open decisions, and scale settles most of the rest

Scale comes first because the others depend on it. The arithmetic runs as follows. With sea level at 63, vanilla's top of 319 leaves 256 blocks above the sea, and a −64..639 overworld (Tectonic's y640 ceiling, 704 blocks) leaves 576. A real alpine range needs 3–5 km of relief. Fitting that with the same scale on both axes needs 3,000/256 ≈ 11.7 to 5,000/256 ≈ 19.5 m per block in a vanilla-height world, but only 3,000/576 ≈ 5.2 to 5,000/576 ≈ 8.7 m per block in the taller one. At 8 m per block the taller world holds 576 × 8 = 4,608 m above sea level, and a 16,384-block world is 131 km across. A median porphyry, a roughly 450 m cube, becomes 56 blocks instead of taller than the whole vanilla world. And the real P50 porphyry density of 15 per 100,000 km² gives 17,180 × 15/100,000 ≈ 2.6 deposits on a fully permissive 16k map, against 0.04 at 1 m per block. The costs of isotropic scale are that strata thinner than 8 m must be exaggerated to one block, that below-sea depth is 127 blocks ≈ 1 km so ocean floors are clamped, and that trees, mobs and buildings are oversized relative to the land.

## Conclusion

The expected hard part turns out to be solved already, and the expected easy part turns out to be the real work. Running credible tectonics and erosion on a gaming PC in minutes is settled by published models, and plugging simulated terrain into a modded 1.21.1 world without breaking biome mods is settled, under MIT, by ReTerraForged. What nobody has built is the step that makes this mod worth making: turning a simulated history into ore that a player can reason their way to, with deposits that explain themselves, clues that point upstream, and COE drills and CDG pumpjacks that agree with the rocks. Ben's engineering budget, and the MVP's exit test, should go there first. The prettiness of the terrain is borrowed from physics that already works.

The research also exposed one choice the brief did not name. The mod's value depends on the player recognising in the world what they watched form, and at 1 m per block they cannot: a 16 km window is a fragment of one mountain front and holds 0.04 porphyry deposits. Treating the world as an 8 m-per-block model, with honest geometry at reduced size, is what lets relief fit, dips stay true, rifts and arcs fit on the map and real ore densities become playable, all at once. It also gives each world a climate identity instead of vanilla's everything-everywhere, which Ben should embrace or offset deliberately rather than discover in playtesting. For MIC, the payoff is a Create pack where geography finally matters, because the copper is in the arc, the zinc and lead are in the platform and the oil is in the basin, and a train line has to connect them.
