# Player Demand and Prior Art for a Geologic-History-Simulation Minecraft Mod

Research date: 2026-09-27. All Modrinth figures pulled live from `api.modrinth.com/v2/project/<slug>` on 2026-09-27 unless noted; CurseForge figures are as reported in web-search snippets of CurseForge pages (I could not query a CurseForge API directly — no key was available in this environment — so those numbers are secondary/approximate and flagged as such).

## Key Question 1: Downloads/followers/last-update for terrain-gen and geology mods — which are active in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
The Minecraft mod ecosystem shows a massive, currently-active market (tens of millions of downloads, same-week updates in September 2026) for mods that make terrain *look* more realistic/dramatic (Terralith, Tectonic, Biomes O' Plenty family), but a much smaller, more marginal market (tens of thousands of downloads, some already stale) for mods that simulate *geology as a mechanic* driving ore placement (Geolosys, Immersive Geology, GeoCraft). The two oldest "erosion-simulated realistic terrain" mods (TerraForged, Realistic Terrain Generation) are both dead on modern Minecraft versions despite historically enormous download counts, and nothing has fully replaced their specific approach.

### Cited Findings
**Terrain-shape mods (large, active):**
- Terralith: 23,275,629 downloads, 8,626 followers, last updated 2026-09-27 (today), supports versions from 1.18.2 up to 26.3 (2026 snapshot naming) — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/terralith), data via [Modrinth API](https://api.modrinth.com/v2/project/terralith)
- Tectonic: 16,549,909 downloads, 7,324 followers, last updated 2026-09-26, supports 1.18.2–26.3 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/tectonic)
- Lithostitched (shared worldgen-feature library many biome mods depend on): 23,253,236 downloads, 1,560 followers, updated 2026-09-26 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/lithostitched)
- Biomes O' Plenty: 36,425,991 downloads, 5,356 followers, updated 2026-09-27 (today) — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/biomes-o-plenty)
- Regions Unexplored: 9,646,829 downloads, 1,875 followers, updated 2026-08-06 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/regions-unexplored)
- Nullscape (End overhaul, same dev family as Terralith): 5,673,496 downloads, 2,739 followers, updated 2026-09-24 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/nullscape)
- William Wythers' Overhauled Overworld (WWOO): 2,024,971 downloads, 1,352 followers, updated 2026-09-16 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/wwoo)
- Terrestria: 2,348,490 downloads, 720 followers, updated 2026-09-18 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/terrestria)
- Oh The Biomes You'll Go: 1,830,414 downloads, 1,304 followers, but **last updated 2024-12-18** — roughly 21 months stale as of 2026-09-27, i.e. effectively abandoned relative to its 2026-active peers — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/biomesyougo)
- Distant Horizons (LOD renderer, lets players see huge finite worlds from a distance without full chunk loading): 35,188,606 downloads, 14,264 followers, updated 2026-09-21 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/distanthorizons)
- Chunky (world pre-generation utility): 18,357,363 downloads, 6,333 followers, updated 2026-07-23 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/chunky)

**Legacy "realistic terrain" mods — historically huge, now dead on current versions:**
- TerraForged: ~11.7–11.8 million downloads on CurseForge; last real update Oct 23, 2022 (alpha for 1.18.2); never officially reached 1.19+ — [CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/terraforged), per web search of the CurseForge page
- Realistic Terrain Generation (RTG): 12.5M+ downloads on CurseForge; not present on Modrinth at all (confirmed via Modrinth search, no hits); latest mainline file is a 1.12.2 snapshot from 2019-09-10; a fan fork "RTG Plus" was last updated 2024-09-29, still only for 1.12.2 — [CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/realistic-terrain-generation)
- Community continuations exist but are tiny: "FreeTerraForged" on Modrinth has only 795 downloads / 18 followers, published 2026-06-15, updated 2026-09-18 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/freeterraforged); "ReTerraforged Unofficial" exists on CurseForge as "Terraforged optimized and brought to modern versions" per search snippet — [CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/reterraforged-unofficial)
- Open Terrain Generator (OTG): the original is one of the oldest Minecraft terrain mods; a fork "suspended development indefinitely due to issues with Mojang" even though it reached 1.19.2; a fresh 2025 continuation "OTG: Open Terrain Generator Continued" has only 1,270 downloads / 2 followers (published 2025-11-22, updated 2025-12-29) — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/open-terrain-generator-continued), [Info Toast retrospective](https://infotoast.org/site/index.php/2022/03/13/openterraingenerator-the-death-of-an-11-year-old-minecraft-mod/)

Terra is infrastructure other people build datapacks on top of, not itself a finished realistic-world or geology product.

**Geology/ore-simulation mods — small niche, mixed activity:**
- TerraFirmaCraft (modern NeoForge-era rewrite): 719,575 downloads, 650 followers, last updated 2026-09-07 (20 days before this research), version list spans 1.7.10 through 1.21.1 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/terrafirmacraft)
- Geolosys ("Data-Driven, Mod-Compatible TerraFirmaCraft-Inspired Ore Generation," per its own description): only 21,911 downloads, 35 followers, **last updated 2025-07-31** and capped at 1.20.1 — no 1.21 port exists as of 2026-09-27 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/geolosys)
- Immersive Geology (ore-processing addon for Immersive Engineering): 25,459 downloads, 88 followers, updated 2026-09-20 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/immersive-geology)
- GeoCraft (aims at "more realistic ore generation" without clumps): 235 downloads, 4 followers, published 2024-12-21 — essentially unnoticed — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/geocraft)
- GregTech CEu Modern: 412,982+ downloads, 233 followers, last updated 2026-04-12 (about 5.5 months before research date — slower cadence than the terrain mods but still within an active-mod window for a deep endgame tech mod) — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/gregtechceu-modern)

**"Preview before you commit" utilities — original abandoned, but demand persists via 2026 successors:**
- World Preview: 1,992,325 downloads, 544 followers, but **stale since 2024-09-05** — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/world-preview)
- World Preview NeoForged (unofficial fork): 21,340 downloads, published 2026-04-01, updated 2026-06-23 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/world-preview-neoforged-(unofficial))
- Genesis – World Previewer & Pregenerator (independent 2026 successor): 23,851 downloads, published 2026-05-25, updated 2026-09-06 (21 days before this research) — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/genesis-world-previewer-pregenerator)

**Build The Earth / real-geodata worldgen mods — small on Modrinth, but the surrounding project is huge (see Key Question 4):**
- TerraPlusPlus (Terra++, the modern Terra 1-to-1 fork used by Build The Earth): only 8,296 downloads, 25 followers on Modrinth, last updated 2021-12-14, capped at 1.12.2 — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/terraplusplus). Note: BTE teams largely distribute this via GitHub/CurseForge/self-hosted server builds rather than a simple Modrinth download, so this figure understates real usage; it should not be read as "BTE has 8,296 users."

### Inferences
- There is roughly a 1,000x gap between "realistic-looking terrain shape" mod adoption (Terralith/Tectonic: tens of millions of downloads, updated same-week) and "geology-as-mechanic" mod adoption (Geolosys/GeoCraft/Immersive Geology: tens of thousands). This suggests the mass audience wants dramatic, good-looking, explorable terrain far more than it currently seeks out simulated geology mechanics — but see Key Question 6 for why this may reflect *supply* (no modern, easy geology-mechanic mod exists at Terralith's polish level) rather than a ceiling on *demand*.
- TerraForged's and RTG's combined ~24M lifetime CurseForge downloads, despite both being dead for years on current Minecraft versions, is the strongest single "vacuum" signal in this whole dataset: an approach (simulated erosion producing realistic-looking terrain) that once drew tens of millions of downloads has no actively-maintained modern equivalent using that specific method — Tectonic/Terralith achieve "realistic-looking" through curated noise/spline shaping tuned for drama and biome compatibility, not through simulating erosion or plate motion.
- The "World Preview" lineage (original dead, two independent successors launched and updated within the last 5 months) is a clean example of a specific niche want — see what you're about to commit to before generating/exploring a big world — persisting and re-forming around abandoned tools, which is directly relevant to Ben's "show the history as it runs" pitch: people already build tools to *preview* worlds; a mod that makes the preview itself a spectacle (a timelapse of formation) has a plausible built-in audience among the same players.

### Gaps
- I could not query the CurseForge API directly (no key configured in this environment), so all CurseForge figures (TerraForged, RTG) come from web-search paraphrases of CurseForge pages rather than a primary JSON fetch; treat as approximate.
- I did not obtain CurseForge-side download counts for Terralith/Tectonic/TFC to add to their Modrinth totals, so the true combined download totals for these mods are higher than what's cited here.
- "World Preview" and "Genesis" download/follower splits by platform (Modrinth vs CurseForge) were not separately checked.

## Key Question 2: Community sentiment on realistic terrain generators, TFC geology/prospecting, GregTech ore veins, vanilla ore distribution

### Takeaway
Sentiment is genuinely split and mechanism-dependent: players reward terrain realism when it stays traversable and beautiful (Tectonic/Terralith's mass popularity), but the same "realistic scarcity/distance" logic applied to resource-finding (TerraFirmaCraft's ore veins, vanilla's post-1.18 ore density) draws recurring complaints about tedium once the novelty wears off. Direct, dated 2025–2026 Reddit sentiment threads were hard to surface through search for the ore-vein-specific questions; most concrete complaint text I found is older (2014–2022) or comes from wikis/forums rather than Reddit itself, which is a real gap.
### Cited Findings
- Sportskeeda (aggregator) on realistic terrain mods: "Veteran players might get bored with vanilla terrain generation since it might feel repetitive after a while, and Tectonic is one of the most popular terrain Minecraft mods" — framing Tectonic explicitly as a cure for vanilla monotony — [Sportskeeda, Tectonic mod guide](https://www.sportskeeda.com/minecraft/minecraft-tectonic-mod-guide-features-download-guide)
- TerraFirmaCraft's own Field Guide confirms the geology mechanic: "rock regions can be over a kilometer across," 2-3 rock layers underfoot at any point, four rock categories (sedimentary/metamorphic/igneous extrusive/igneous intrusive), and "different ores are found in different rock types, so locating specific rock types can be very important" — [TFC Field Guide, Geology](https://terrafirmacraft.github.io/Field-Guide/en_us/the_world/geology.html)
- Player complaints about TFC's pacing, sourced from Feed The Beast forum discussion ("TerraFirmaCraft opinions?"): players "love playing TerraFirmaCraft early on" but find it "annoying once they reach steel progression due to the grind"; one commenter said progression felt "too slow," citing having "to go thousands of blocks to find a nickel vein" — [FTB Forum thread](https://forum.feed-the-beast.com/threads/terrafirmacraft-opinions.51111/) (thread content appears to date to the mid-2010s TFC era, not 2025-2026 — caveat below)
- On vanilla ore distribution: after the 1.18 Caves & Cliffs update, players complained "the density of underground resources... was too low," that "the number of levels doubled but resources roughly similar to pre-1.18, requiring mining much more volume for the same amount of resources," and that strip mining, while effective, was "tedious" — [Minecraft Feedback, "Resource density too low"](https://feedback.minecraft.net/hc/en-us/community/posts/360077630552-Resource-density-too-low); ore Y-levels/spawn rates have not changed since 1.18 through 1.21 per the same search summary.
- GregTech New Horizons (GTNH) documents its ore-vein system as a deliberate alternative to vanilla strip-mining: veins contain "several hundred ores," generate three chunks apart, hold only 1-4 ore types each (varying by dimension/planet), and the intended play pattern is "finding each of the unique ore veins... and marking their locations on a map to repeatedly come back to them, with no strip mining as in vanilla Minecraft" — [GT New Horizons Wiki, Ore Generation](https://wiki.gtnewhorizons.com/wiki/Ore_Generation)
- Evidence that vein-based ore systems are polarizing rather than universally loved: "GregNothing," a mod/config whose entire purpose is to disable GregTech's ore generation, exists and is maintained on GitHub — [GregNothing](https://github.com/FrederikRasmussen/GregNothing) — implying a real subset of players who want the GregTech machine/processing chain without its imposed ore-finding mechanic.
- Old (2020) GregTech ore-vein config discussion, still open, shows the vein layout system is an ongoing point of tuning/contention among modpack devs — [GregTechCE GitHub issue #38](https://github.com/GregTechCE/GregTech/issues/38)

### Inferences
- The pattern across TFC, GregTech/GTNH, and vanilla post-1.18 is consistent: players broadly accept — and even ask for — realistic-feeling, geography-tied resource scarcity as a *novel, early-game* hook, but the same mechanic reads as "tedious" once it gates late-game progression or forces long, undirected travel with no informational aid. A geologic-history mod that wants ore-genesis realism without inheriting this complaint would need to pair scarcity with *information* (a way to reason about or survey where ore should be, echoing GTNH's "mark it on a map" workflow) rather than pure trial-and-error digging.
- The vanilla ore-distribution complaints are really about *density/tedium*, not about realism versus randomness per se — this is a distinct axis from what Ben's mod would change (WHERE ores are, tied to real geology) and suggests his approach could either fix the vanilla complaint (giving mining a legible "there's iron under sedimentary basins" logic instead of undirected Y-level digging) or worsen it (if the geology model makes some ores genuinely rare/regional and travel-gated).
### Gaps
- I could not find dated, specific 2025–2026 Reddit threads (r/feedthebeast, r/Minecraft, r/TerraFirmaCraft) with sentiment on GregTech ore veins or current TFC opinions — search kept surfacing GitHub issues, wikis, and old (2014-era) forum posts instead of live Reddit discussion. This is a real gap in the assignment's requested sourcing (Reddit specifically) that a session with direct Reddit search/API access could close.
- No quantified sentiment (e.g., upvote ratios, comment counts) was found for any of these topics — all findings above are qualitative snippets from forums/wikis, not measured sentiment.

## Key Question 3: Analogues outside Minecraft (Dwarf Fortress, WorldBox, Undiscovered Worlds, Azgaar's, Gaea/World Machine)

### Takeaway
Every analogue checked confirms that "simulate a world's history, then hand the player a world shaped by that history" is a proven, commercially successful pattern (Dwarf Fortress: 1M+ Steam sales, ~$19.9M gross; WorldBox: ~96% positive over tens of thousands of reviews) — and one analogue, "Undiscovered Worlds," is close to a direct blueprint for Ben's simulation stack (plate tectonics → mountain building → volcanism → erosion → deposition) outside Minecraft entirely.
### Cited Findings
- Dwarf Fortress worldgen: "Mountains will form and be eroded, forests will spread, rivers will spawn, civilisations will rise and fall," alongside "a dynamic weather model that tracks wind, humidity, and air masses"; the appeal is explicitly that history "feels like it happened and happens for a reason, not just that it's scripted," because it emerges from "a giant zero-player strategy game... with thousands of agents" — [search-cited summary drawing on Dwarf Fortress Wiki, world generation](https://www.dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/World_generation) and [df-walkthrough tutorial](https://df-walkthrough.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/generating-a-world.html)
- Dwarf Fortress commercial performance (Steam release): sales passed 300,000 in six days, 600,000 by February 2023, 800,000 by April 2024, and have since surpassed 1,000,000 copies; VG Insights estimates ~$19.9M gross revenue on Steam; peak concurrent players hit 3,406 on 2024-04-17; estimated owners range 2,000,000–5,000,000 — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/04/04/dwarf-fortress-sales-800k-steam-1-million-wishlists), [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/04/dwarf-fortress-hits-800000-sales-and-no-sign-of-it-stopping/), [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-47594)
- WorldBox – God Simulator (Steam): "96% of the 29,730 user reviews for this game are positive" per one aggregator; a second aggregator (Steambase) reports "a Player Score of 95/100 from 56,244 total reviews" (an "Overwhelmingly Positive" rating) — the review-count discrepancy likely reflects different snapshot dates/methodologies between the two aggregators — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1206560/WorldBox__God_Simulator/), [Steambase](https://steambase.io/games/worldbox-god-simulator/reviews)
- WorldBox player feedback: "players enjoying the ability to shape worlds and watch civilizations develop, though some mention the game can become repetitive after extended play" — [search-cited summary]
- Undiscovered Worlds is described (project GitHub) as "a world engine for generating a planet's physical environment and simulating the emergence of ecosystems, settlements, and political geography," whose output is "a world snapshot with enough natural and social history to explain its terrain, resources, population, and borders." Mechanically: "The engine combines procedural heuristics with more physically based simulations. It runs plate tectonics, then selected terraforming passes: mountain building, volcanism, coastline refinement, erosion, and deposition." It ships in two forms: a spherical/orbit-navigable planet version, and a "classic" version that imports a user's own map and simulates climate/rivers/lakes onto it — [GitHub, lukabergs/UndiscoveredWorlds](https://github.com/lukabergs/UndiscoveredWorlds), [GitHub, JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds](https://github.com/JonathanCRH/Undiscovered_Worlds)
- Azgaar's Fantasy Map Generator: open-source, browser-based, "has long been the go-to for fantasy writers and Dungeons and Dragons players alike"; generates a "realistic map with one click based on real features like elevation, sea level, latitude, precipitation and more," then auto-populates "states, cities, religions and populations... based on the climate and topography"; 5,538 GitHub stars and 870 forks — [Roll20 GM Hub](https://gmhub.roll20.net/resources/azgaar-fantasy-map-generator/), [GitHub repo, cited via Reedsy blog](https://reedsy.com/blog/fantasy-map-generators/)
- Gaea (QuadSpinner) and World Machine are both described as general-purpose procedural terrain-design tools "for VFX, games, and virtual production," used by some Minecraft map-makers via a Gaea → PNG heightmap → WorldPainter import pipeline — [QuadSpinner](https://quadspinner.com/), [Info Toast, "How to Use Quadspinner Gaea for Minecraft"](https://infotoast.org/site/index.php/2023/03/06/how-to-use-quadspinner-gaea-for-minecraft/), [Planet Minecraft forum, "World Painter + Gaea Terrain Generation"](https://www.planetminecraft.com/forums/communities/mapping/world-painter-gaea-terrain-generation-render-dump-before-deleting-678409/)

### Inferences
- Across all four non-Minecraft analogues, the thing users explicitly value is not realism for its own sake but *legible causality*: Dwarf Fortress fans prize that history "happened for a reason"; Azgaar's auto-populates plausible civilizations from the terrain it just made; Undiscovered Worlds' stated design goal is a world whose "terrain, resources, population, and borders" are all explained by its own history. This directly supports Ben's premise that the timelapse/history-sim framing (not just a better heightmap) is the differentiated value proposition, and that ore genesis tied causally to the simulated geology (not just placed to "look right") is the natural equivalent of Dwarf Fortress's legends.
- Undiscovered Worlds is close to prior art for Ben's *simulation pipeline specifically* (plate tectonics → mountain building → volcanism → erosion → deposition) even though it's not a Minecraft mod and doesn't do ore genesis — worth Ben looking at its source/approach directly as a reference implementation.
- Gaea/World Machine → WorldPainter is evidence that Minecraft map-makers already reach for real geoscience-adjacent procedural tools when they want realism, but today that pipeline produces a static heightmap only (no simulated rock layers, no ore genesis, no watchable timelapse) — a gap Ben's mod would fill if it could plug into or outclass this workflow.

### Gaps
- I did not find usage/download numbers for Gaea or World Machine specific to their Minecraft-mapmaking use (as opposed to their primary VFX/games markets), only qualitative tutorial evidence that the workflow exists and is documented.
- I did not find follower/star counts or user-base size for "Undiscovered Worlds" itself (its GitHub repos didn't surface star counts in the search snippets), so I can't quantify its adoption, only describe its design as a close analogue.

## Key Question 4: Finite/bounded worlds — map-based servers, WorldPainter/Gaea SMP maps, world-border survival, demand for fresh realistic continents

### Takeaway
Finite, bordered, realistically-shaped Minecraft worlds are an established, multi-sided market today: long-running "Earth" geopolitics servers (10 years for EarthMC), a real commercial micro-market of hand-built "massive and realistic" custom continents sized in the tens of thousands of blocks sold specifically for survival/MMORPG servers, and the Build The Earth project's use of real geodata at 1:1 scale. All of this demand is currently served by either (a) real-world map data, or (b) one-off hand-painted/heightmap-imported terrain — none of it is procedurally regenerated from a simulated geologic history, which is exactly the gap Ben's mod would fill.
### Cited Findings
- EarthMC: "Founded in 2016," billed as "the original earth server," running Towny-based nation/town gameplay on "an exclusive Minecraft earth map... rendered using satellite height and terrain data, allowing players to visit their real life home" — [EarthMC](https://earthmc.net/)
- DiplomaticaMC: geopolitics server on a "1:450 Earth map," reporting "12,000+ unique users" — [DiplomaticaMC](https://diplomaticamc.com/)
- CCNet Nations: geopolitical server "set on a 1:1000 scale map of Earth" combining "conflict, diplomacy, trade and a player-driven economy, shipbuilding and more" — [TopG server listing](https://topg.org/minecraft-servers/type/Geopolitical/)
- Terradivisa: geopolitical earth server where "the only way to obtain ores is from capturing resource sites from other nations, or forging diplomatic ties to trade with them" — i.e., an existing server design that already makes *geography-bound resource control* the central diplomatic mechanic — [TopG server listing](https://topg.org/minecraft-servers/type/Geopolitical/)
- Build The Earth (BTE): a global volunteer project (founded March 2020) to recreate the whole planet in Minecraft at 1:1 scale, organized into "45 teams ranging from a handful of people to thousands of members"; its largest single team (New York City) has "over 3,719 members" and after 5 years has produced "over 50,000 buildings" across roughly 700 km² — [Wikipedia, Build the Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Build_the_Earth), [Tom's Hardware, NYC 1:1](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/pc-gaming/new-york-city-recreated-at-1-1-scale-inside-minecraft-project-took-5-whole-years-and-contains-over-50-000-buildings)
- The BTE technical stack (Terra 1-to-1 → TerraPlusPlus) pulls "terrain, biomes, trees, and roads... from real global data sources" (OpenStreetMap for roads, public terrain datasets for elevation/biome), and uses CubicChunks to extend the vertical build range beyond vanilla's 256-block limit to accommodate Everest-to-Mariana-Trench relief — [GitHub, terra121 README](https://github.com/orangeadam3/terra121/blob/master/README.md), [GitHub, BuildTheEarth/terraplusplus wiki](https://github.com/BuildTheEarth/terraplusplus/wiki)
- A real commercial/gift-economy micro-market exists for hand-built finite "realistic" continents explicitly for survival: "Terra-Titan 1.20/1.21 – Massive and Realistic 30,000 x 30,000 Map | Survival Friendly/MMORPG-Focused" and "Terra-Mar 1.20/1.21 – Massive and Realistic 40,000 x 40,000 Map" are both listed on Planet Minecraft, explicitly targeting survival and MMORPG-style servers — [Planet Minecraft, Terra-Titan](https://www.planetminecraft.com/project/terra-titan-1-20-1-21-massive-and-realistic-30-000-x-30-000-map-survival-friendly-mmorpg-focused-java-edition/), [Planet Minecraft, Terra-Mar](https://www.planetminecraft.com/project/terra-mar-1-20-massive-and-realistic-40-000-x-40-000-map-survival-friendly-mmorpg-focused-java-edition/)
- Smaller-scale paid custom terrain: "The Varuna Store" sells a "2K Realistic Minecraft Terrain Map" (2,000×2,000 blocks); BuiltByBit lists WorldPainter "Custom Map Commissions" starting around £5–$7 scaling with size/detail — [Varuna Store](https://store.varunabuilds.com/p/2k-minecraft-realistic-terrain-map/), [BuiltByBit thread](https://builtbybit.com/threads/custom-map-commissions-world-painter.33369/)
- Denmark built a full 1:1 recreation of the entire country in Minecraft in 2014 using official Danish Geodata Agency elevation data (~4,000 billion blocks total), explicitly as "an invitation to teachers and schools to use the data in geography, math, science and history lessons"; the map subsequently had to cope with heavy public traffic and griefing after release — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/denmark-creates-11-scale-replica-of-itself-in-minecraft/), [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2014/04/30/denmark-writes-minecraft-world-history-with-official-map-data/), [Engadget, "why we can't have nice things"](https://www.engadget.com/2014-05-11-denmark-made-in-minecraft-is-why-we-cant-have-nice-things.html)
- WorldPainter's own mapping/worldbuilding Discord community has "4,846 members" (for comparison, the more general-purpose "World Creator" terrain-tool Discord has 13,477) — [search-cited Discord invite listings]

### Inferences
- The existence of a paid market for one-off "massive and realistic 30,000×30,000 / 40,000×40,000" survival continents (Terra-Titan, Terra-Mar) is close to direct proof of concept for Ben's target output (a finite, bordered, realistically-shaped world of "thousands to tens of thousands of blocks across"): players are already paying for exactly this *shape* of product, just without any simulated geologic mechanism behind it and without regeneratability (buy one map, that's the one map).
- The Earth-geopolitics server niche (EarthMC, DiplomaticaMC, CCNet, Terradivisa) shows sustained (EarthMC: 10 years) demand for finite worlds whose *geography drives politics and resource access* — Terradivisa in particular already runs a "control the ore-bearing territory" diplomacy loop manually on a copied real-world map; a mod that generates a fresh, non-Earth continent with geologically real ore distribution would let server owners run this same design without depending on real-world geography or OpenStreetMap licensing/accuracy constraints.
- All of this finite-world demand today is met either by (a) copying the real Earth (BTE, EarthMC-style servers) or (b) manual/heightmap-based one-off art (Terra-Titan, WorldPainter commissions) — nobody currently offers "procedurally generate a new, finite, geologically coherent fictional world, watch it form, then play it," which is precisely Ben's proposed niche.

### Gaps
- I found no data on how many servers actually enforce hard world-borders in practice for "vanilla-style" (non-Earth-map) finite survival, versus just capping normal infinite generation loosely — the Chunky/world-border tooling exists and is well-documented (see Key Question 7) but I don't have server-count adoption stats.
- No pricing/sales-volume data for the Terra-Titan/Terra-Mar/Varuna Store custom-map micro-market was available from search snippets (Planet Minecraft doesn't surface download/purchase counts in search results the way Modrinth's API does) — I could not quantify the size of this micro-market, only confirm it exists and is multi-vendor.

## Key Question 5: Educational/institutional interest in geology + Minecraft

### Takeaway
Institutional interest is real, multi-country, and spans over a decade (2014–2026), including two national geographic/geological survey agencies (UK's Ordnance Survey and British Geological Survey, Denmark's Geodata Agency) and a recurring academic literature on using Minecraft to teach geoscience — but the literature itself flags that Minecraft is *not* well-integrated into formal college-level geology curricula in the US, which tempers how far "institutional demand" actually reaches.
### Cited Findings
- Ordnance Survey (UK) built a Minecraft map of Great Britain from open OS data; the original covered "220,000 square kilometres of mainland Great Britain and surrounding islands" across "22 billion blocks" (from OS Terrain 50, a 50m-resolution digital terrain model, plus OS VectorMap District for water/woodland/roads); it was later "upscaled to a total of 83 billion blocks," each block now representing 25 metres — [Geospatial World](https://geospatialworld.net/news/ordnance-survey-creates-minecraft-map-of-great-britain/), [Ordnance Survey blog](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/blog/minecraft-maps-continue-to-grow), [Geography Realm](https://www.geographyrealm.com/map-great-britain-created-using-minecraft/)
- British Geological Survey (BGS) built directly on the OS map: it "reproduced the 2D geology of mainland Great Britain and surrounding islands within Minecraft, showing the OS map data on the surface and the rough position of real geology beneath, repeated down to the bedrock," plus genuine 3D geology models at three named sites (York, Ingleborough, West Thurrock) "showing how the geology rises and falls, overlaps and folds at different depths" — [BGS, GB geology with OS for Minecraft](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/download/bgs-gb-geology-with-os-for-minecraft/), [BGS, GB geology Minecraft world](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/discovering-geology/maps-and-resources/maps/gb-geology-minecraft-world/)
- Denmark (2014): the Danish Geodata Agency built a full-country 1:1 Minecraft recreation "designed to 'use the appeal of gaming to draw the public's attention to geographical data' and as 'an invitation to teachers and schools to use the data in geography, math, science and history lessons'" — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/denmark-creates-11-scale-replica-of-itself-in-minecraft/), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/video-games/denmark-recreates-itself-minecraft-official-map-data-n89991)
- Academic literature, dated: "The Geology of Minecraft," *Teaching Science*, 2017 — [ERIC EJ1142179](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1142179); "Pandemic Minecrafting: an analysis of the perceptions of and lessons learned from a gamified virtual geology field camp," *Geoscience Communication*, 2021 — [Copernicus GC](https://gc.copernicus.org/articles/4/475/2021/); GSA Connects 2022 conference abstract, "Using Blocks to Teach Rocks: Developing Minecraft Geology Labs" — [GSA Connects](https://gsa.confex.com/gsa/2022AM/webprogram/Paper379255.html); "The Minecraft Education – A Game-Based Platform and its Applications in Geology Education at Lower Secondary School Level (ISCED 2)" (2024) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385625277_THE_MINECRAFT_EDUCATION_-_A_GAME-BASED_PLATFORM_AND_ITS_APPLICATIONS_IN_GEOLOGY_EDUCATION_AT_LOWER_SECONDARY_SCHOOL_LEVEL_ISCED_2); BRGM (the French geological survey) published "Making geosciences fun with Minecraft" — [BRGM](https://www.brgm.fr/en/news/article/making-geosciences-fun-with-minecraft); AGU/Eos coverage, "Digging Deep into Geosciences with Minecraft" — [Eos.org](https://eos.org/science-updates/digging-deep-into-geosciences-with-minecraft)
- A countervailing/limiting finding from the same search: "despite its utilization globally, Minecraft is not regularly incorporated in college-level geology courses in the United States; only one activity using Minecraft is published on the Science Education Resource Center website" — and prior research on Minecraft-as-teaching-tool has centered on "elementary and secondary learners," with post-secondary geoscience applications "scarce" — [search-cited academic summary, referencing the SERC-published virtual field experience aligned to NAGT outcomes]
- A Japanese case study: researchers "constructed a virtual Minecraft world for a junior high school... that included the actual school buildings and underground geologic strata based on boring [drilling] data, as well as virtual worlds of paleoenvironments to help students understand geological history" — [search-cited, from the same Minecraft-geoscience-education search batch]

### Inferences
- The BGS's GB-geology Minecraft map is the closest existing institutional artifact to "a Minecraft world with real geologic structure," but it is static, non-procedural, and tied to real-Earth data rather than a generated fictional world — it proves the *display* half of Ben's concept (rock layers folding and varying with depth, visualized in Minecraft) is something a national geological survey judged worth building for outreach, which is independent validation that geologically structured Minecraft terrain has didactic value.
- The 2017–2024 span of academic papers shows sustained (not one-off) interest in Minecraft-for-geoscience-education, but the explicit note that US college geology courses rarely use Minecraft suggests any educational pitch for Ben's mod should target K-12 outreach and geological-survey-style public engagement (where there's a track record) rather than assume ready-made university course adoption.

### Gaps
- I did not find usage/download numbers for the BGS or OS Minecraft maps (e.g., how many times they were downloaded, or evidence they're still actively used/maintained in 2025-2026 — both projects date to roughly the mid-2010s per available snippets, and I did not confirm whether they've been updated or are still distributed today).
- I did not find any Minecraft Education Edition-specific geology lesson plans/marketplace listings with usage numbers, despite searching in this area — the 2024 ResearchGate paper on Minecraft Education in geology touches this but I only have the abstract-level framing, not adoption figures.

## Key Question 6: Unmet-demand signals (explicit requests for plate-tectonics worldgen, realistic ore gen, "TerraForged for 1.21," etc.)

### Takeaway
There is a long paper trail of players explicitly asking for tectonics-driven Minecraft world generation going back to at least the early Minecraft Forum era, met at the time with the specific objection that it would be "impossible to code" without rewriting Minecraft's world engine from scratch — which is exactly the kind of standalone, pre-generation-time tool Ben is proposing, sidestepping that objection. Layered on top, TerraForged's death and its multiple small, ongoing 2026 fan-revival attempts (FreeTerraForged, ReTerraforged Unofficial) are a direct, dated signal of unmet demand specifically for a modern, actively maintained "realistic terrain" mod.
### Cited Findings
- An old Minecraft Forum thread is literally titled "[Idea] MINECRAFT - Tectonic Plates Mod! |VOLCANOES, CAVE INS AND MORE|" proposing convergent/divergent/transform plate boundaries producing mountains, trenches, splitting landmasses, and volcanoes over time — [Minecraft Forum](https://www.minecraftforum.net/forums/mapping-and-modding-java-edition/minecraft-mods/requests-ideas-for-mods/1328878-idea-minecraft-tectonic-plates-mod-volcanoes-cave)
- A separate Minecraft Forum suggestions thread, "Plate tectonics in Minecraft," shows the same request made directly to Mojang as a vanilla suggestion — [Minecraft Forum](https://www.minecraftforum.net/forums/minecraft-java-edition/suggestions/55671-plate-tectonics-in-minecraft)
- Per the search summary of these threads, responses at the time argued such a feature "would be impossible to code and would require completely wiping Minecraft and starting from scratch" — i.e., the community's own objection was about retrofitting *live, infinite* vanilla worldgen, not about a separate pre-generation tool producing a finite world (Ben's actual proposed architecture).
- TerraForged's death (last real update October 2022, per Key Question 1) combined with two independent, currently-updating 2026 fan-revival projects — "FreeTerraForged" (published 2026-06-15, updated 2026-09-18) and "ReTerraforged Unofficial" (per CurseForge search snippet, "Terraforged optimized and brought to modern versions") — is a dated, concrete signal that demand for TerraForged's specific approach has not gone away four years after abandonment, even though neither revival has meaningfully replaced it yet (FreeTerraForged: 795 downloads as of 2026-09-27) — [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/freeterraforged), [CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/reterraforged-unofficial)
- Geolosys explicitly frames itself as filling a gap: "Data-Driven, Mod-Compatible TerraFirmaCraft-Inspired Ore Generation" — i.e., built specifically so modpacks can get TFC-style geological ore placement *without* adopting all of TFC — but as shown in Key Question 1 it has stalled at 1.20.1 with no 1.21 port and only 21,911 downloads, suggesting the demand for "TFC-style ore logic, lighter-weight" exists but is currently underserved even by the mod built to serve it.
- The "World Preview" lineage (Key Question 1) — original abandoned 2024-09-05, two independent successors launched in 2026 — is itself an unmet-demand signal, adjacent to Ben's "watch the world take shape" pitch: players want visibility into a world before/while committing to it, and will rebuild the tooling for that from scratch when it lapses.

### Inferences
- The specific, decade-plus-old "impossible to code" objection to plate tectonics in Minecraft is actually favorable evidence for Ben's architecture: by simulating geologic history *offline*, in a separate tool, and then handing Minecraft a finished, static, finite world (rather than trying to run live tectonics inside Minecraft's infinite, always-generating world engine), Ben's proposed design specifically avoids the technical objection that killed this idea as a vanilla/live-worldgen feature.
- Taken together, TerraForged's zombie afterlife and Geolosys's stalled-but-still-attempted niche both point to the same conclusion: there is standing, unresolved demand for both halves of Ben's pitch (realistic terrain *shape* and geology-driven *ore placement*) separately, but no current mod combines them, and the mods that tried each half individually have both stalled out technically (TerraForged: abandoned; Geolosys: unported to 1.21) rather than being killed by lack of interest.

### Gaps
- I did not find a modern (2024-2026), high-signal Reddit or forum thread with someone explicitly typing "I wish there was a plate tectonics mod for [current version]" — the historical Forum threads found are old (dates not fully confirmed from snippets, but Minecraft-Forum-era phrasing suggests pre-2016), so I cannot confirm this exact ask is still being voiced verbatim in 2025-2026, only that its two structural sub-asks (better terrain shape, better ore logic) both show live, dated 2026 successor/revival activity.
- I did not find search-visible evidence of people specifically typing "TerraForged for 1.21" as a request (the search instead surfaced the fan-revival projects themselves, which is suggestive but not a direct quote of the request).

## Key Question 7: Risks and counter-evidence (faded projects, tolerance for long world-creation times, multiplayer/server friction)

### Takeaway
The clearest concrete risks are: (1) legal/technical fragility for mods that hook deep into world generation (OTG's fork was suspended "due to issues with Mojang"); (2) a real tedium failure mode when realism-driven scarcity isn't paired with tools to reason about it (TFC's late-game grind complaints, vanilla's post-1.18 density complaints); and (3) genuine friction from large, real-data worlds in multiplayer (Denmark's map faced griefing/traffic problems). Counterbalancing this, long pre-generation/world-creation times are not, by themselves, a demonstrated dealbreaker — the server community has built and normalized tooling (Chunky, world borders) specifically to accept and manage multi-hour-to-multi-day generation waits.
### Cited Findings
- OTG's fork "indefinitely suspended development due to issues with Mojang, even though it works up to 1.19.2" — a specific, named legal/policy risk for mods that patch deep into Minecraft's world-generation internals — [search-cited], background on OTG's broader decline in [Info Toast, "OpenTerrainGenerator: The Death of an 11-Year-Old Minecraft Mod"](https://infotoast.org/site/index.php/2022/03/13/openterraingenerator-the-death-of-an-11-year-old-minecraft-mod/)
- TerraFirmaCraft grind complaints (Key Question 2): players "love playing... early on" but the same geology-gated-resource design becomes "annoying" and drives people to "lose interest" once it gates late-game progression — [FTB Forum](https://forum.feed-the-beast.com/threads/terrafirmacraft-opinions.51111/)
- Vanilla post-1.18 ore density complaints (Key Question 2) show that even Mojang's own team can misjudge the tedium threshold when changing how deep/spread-out resource-finding is — [Minecraft Feedback](https://feedback.minecraft.net/hc/en-us/community/posts/360077630552-Resource-density-too-low)
- Denmark's 1:1 map: after public release "why we can't have nice things" — the map had to deal with griefing and heavy demand/traffic as a direct consequence of being large, real, and open to the public — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-05-11-denmark-made-in-minecraft-is-why-we-cant-have-nice-things.html)
- Pre-generation time is a known, quantified, and already-tooled-for cost, not an open risk: "A 5,000-block radius takes 1-3 hours on typical hardware... For large areas (radius over 10,000 blocks), pregeneration can take dozens of hours," and the accepted server practice is to "pre-generate before opening to players, then set a world border, then open to players," ideally run overnight or throttled alongside play — [search-cited guide summary, drawing on Chunky documentation](https://github.com/pop4959/Chunky/wiki/Pregeneration) and hosting-provider guides (WinterNode, SuperCraft, Space-Node)
- OTG and RTG both show that even extremely popular terrain mods (12M+ downloads each) can stall for years across a single Minecraft version jump (RTG never left 1.12.2/1.7.10 in any meaningful way; TerraForged never left 1.18.2) — evidence that terrain-generation mods specifically carry high multi-version maintenance risk, likely because deep worldgen hooks are exactly the kind of code that breaks hardest on each new Minecraft/mod-loader release.
- WorldBox's own review commentary flags a genre-relevant risk even in a beloved god-sim: "the game can become repetitive after extended play" — [search-cited summary of Steam reviews]

### Inferences
- The multiplayer/server-friction risk (Denmark) is really about *hosting a single shared real-world-data map at massive public scale*, which is not quite Ben's use case (a modpack where each server/player generates their own finite fictional world) — so this specific risk is more attenuated for MIC's use case than for a "one canonical shared Earth server" design, but would still apply if Ben's mod produces a "showcase" world that many people expect to explore on one shared server.
- The recurring pattern of terrain-gen mods stalling across Minecraft version jumps (OTG, TerraForged, RTG all frozen on old versions) is the single most concrete, mechanism-level risk for Ben's own mod: a plate-tectonics/erosion/ore-genesis simulator is, if anything, a deeper and more version-coupled piece of worldgen engineering than any of these, so version-migration cost should be budgeted as a first-class risk, not an afterthought.
- Long world-creation times are de-risked by existing norms (Chunky + world border, run overnight) as long as Ben's mod is used the way servers already use pre-generation — i.e., as a one-time setup cost before players ever see the world — rather than something individual players sit and wait on interactively; the "watch it happen" timelapse framing actually helps here by turning the necessary compute time into the marketed feature rather than a hidden cost.

### Gaps
- I found no direct evidence (reviews, forum posts) of players complaining specifically about *long world-generation wait times* as a reason to dislike a Minecraft mod or server — the only friction found was indirect (via pregeneration guides describing how to manage/hide the cost), so I cannot confirm or rule out that very long (many-hour) waits have ever caused a documented backlash for a Minecraft product specifically.
- I did not find a documented case of a Minecraft mod/project that failed *specifically because* its finite/bounded-world premise (as opposed to technical/legal/maintenance reasons) turned players off — all the "faded project" evidence I found (TerraForged, RTG, OTG) points to maintenance/version-migration and, in OTG's case, legal pressure, as the cause of decline, not lack of player interest in the underlying concept.
